1
Canon 1 If anyone says that a man can justify himself before God by his works, done or by the forces of human nature or by the doctrine of the law, without divine grace through Jesus Christ: let him be anathema.

2
Canon 2 If anyone says that divine grace through Jesus Christ is given only so that man can live more easily with justice and deserve eternal life, as if by free will without grace he could do both, even with difficulty and labor: let him be anathema.

4
Sessio 4 The sacred ecumenical and general synod of Trent, legitimately gathered in the Holy Spirit, presided over therein by the same three legates of the Apostolic See, perpetually proposing before our eyes this: that, errors removed, the very purity of the Gospel be preserved in the Church: which, promised before by the Prophets in the holy Scriptures, our Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, first promulgated with his own mouth, and then commanded that it be preached to every creature by his Apostles as a source of all salvific truth and discipline of customs; and seeing that this truth and discipline are contained in the written books and in the unwritten traditions, which, received from the mouth of Christ himself by the Apostles, or by the Apostles themselves dictated by the Holy Spirit, as they have come down to us from hand to hand: following the examples of the Orthodox Fathers, he receives and venerates with equal affection of piety and reverence all the books of both the Old and the New Testament, since of both there is only one God the author, and also the same traditions, since belonging to faith, or to customs, as dictated or spoken by Christ, or by the Holy Spirit, and preserved in the Catholic Church by continuous succession.

5
Session 5 If anyone does not confess that the first man Adam, when in paradise he had transgressed the command of God, immediately lost the holiness and justice in which he had been constituted, and incurred by the offense of this prevarication the wrath and indignation of God, and therefore in the death, which God had previously threatened him, and with death in captivity under the power of him who afterwards had the empire of death, that is, of the devil, and that all Adam for that offense of prevarication was changed for the worse according to body and soul: let him be accursed.

6
Sessio 6 Further declares that this is the free justification of Jesus Christ: not only the remission of sins, but also the sanctification and renewal of the inner man by the voluntary reception of grace and gifts. From where the unjust man becomes righteous, and the enemy a friend, so that he may be an heir according to the hope of eternal life.
