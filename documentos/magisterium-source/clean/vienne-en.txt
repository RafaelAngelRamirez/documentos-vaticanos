Canon 1 on the Order of the Templars For perpetual memory of the fact. It is up to the solicitous providence of the Vicar of Christ to suppress things that are scandalous in the Church of God, and to provide for the usefulness of faith and the Holy Land. For serious and well-known causes exposed before us and the sacred council, with the approval of the sacred council, we suppress, annul, reprove and perpetually interdict the Order of the Templar militia, not by way of definitive sentence, but by way of provision and apostolic ordination, strictly prohibiting anyone from now on from entering said order, or taking its habit, or daring to behave as a Templar. We apply and want the goods of said order to be considered applied to the use of faith and the relief of the Holy Land and other pious places, according to what the Apostolic See has decided to order.

2
Canon 2 on the soul as a form of the body Furthermore, with the approval of the foretold sacred Council, we reprove as erroneous and enemy of the truth of the Catholic faith any doctrine or position that recklessly affirms, or casts doubt, that the substance of the rational or intellective soul is not truly and in itself the form of the human body, defining - so that the truth of sincere faith is known to all and access is closed to all errors, so that they do not enter - that whoever from now on dares to affirm, defend or maintain with pertinacity that the rational or intellective soul is not a form of the human body in itself and essentially, must be considered a heretic.

Canon 1 on the Order of the Templars For perpetual memory of the fact. 3
Canon 3 Beguines and beguines and others who, under the appearance of holiness, spread errors about the spirit of freedom and impeccability, if they do not repent, we separate them from the communion of the faithful.

4
Canon 4 General studies and theological schools should be encouraged wherever possible, so that faith and science may grow among the Christian people.

5
Canon 5 The tithes and oblations that correspond to the churches must not be usurped by the laity. Those who retain what has been usurped are compelled to restitution through censorship.

6
Canon 6 Prelates do not neglect the visits and corrections of their subjects, nor impose excessive taxes.

7
Canon 7 Usury and illicit contracts are to be avoided entirely by Christians. The Jews are also restrained from immoderate taxes and usury against the Christians, justice is saved.

8
Canon 8 Religious must not be admitted to the profession until after a due period of probation, nor must they leave the monastery without permission from their superior.

9
Canon 9 To the heretics and their defenders, if they have not returned to the unity of the faith, we wound them with anathema.

10
Canon 10 We command that these things, established by the holy synod of Vienne, be inviolably observed by all.
