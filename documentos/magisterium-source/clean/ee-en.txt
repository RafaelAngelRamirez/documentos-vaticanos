1. The Church lives from the Eucharist. This truth not only expresses a daily experience of faith, but also contains in summary the core of the mystery of the Church. She experiences with joy how the Lord's promise is continually realized, in multiple forms: "Behold, I am with you always to the end of the world" ([+[0]+]); In the sacred Eucharist, through the transformation of the bread and wine into the body and blood of the Lord, he rejoices in this presence with a unique intensity. Since, on Pentecost, the Church, People of the New Alliance, has begun its pilgrimage towards the heavenly homeland, this divine Sacrament has marked its days, filling them with confident hope.

2. During the Great Jubilee of the year 2000, I had the opportunity to celebrate the Eucharist in the Cenacle of Jerusalem, where, according to tradition, it was performed for the first time by Christ himself. The Cenacle is the place of the institution of this Most Holy Sacrament. There Christ took the bread in his hands, broke it and gave it to the disciples saying: "Take it, all of you, and eat it, for this is my Body, which will be given for you" ([+[0]+]). Then he took the chalice of wine in their hands and said to them: "Take and drink of it, all of you, for this is the chalice of my blood, the blood of the new and eternal covenant, which will be poured out for you and for all men for the forgiveness of sins" ([+[1]+]). I am grateful to the Lord Jesus who allowed me to repeat in that same place, obeying his command "do this in memory of me" ([+[2]+]), the words spoken by Him two thousand years ago.

3
3. From the paschal mystery the Church is born. Precisely for this reason the Eucharist, which is the sacrament par excellence of the paschal mystery, is at the center of ecclesial life. This can already be observed from the first images of the Church that the Acts of the Apostles offer us: "They attended assiduously to the teaching of the apostles, to communion, to the breaking of bread and to the prayers" ([+[0]+]). The "breaking of bread" evokes the Eucharist. After two thousand years we continue to reproduce that original image of the Church. And, while we do so in the Eucharistic celebration, the eyes of the soul are directed to the Easter Triduum: to what happened on the afternoon of Holy Thursday, during the Last Supper and after it. The institution of the Eucharist, in effect, sacramentally anticipated the events that would take place a little later, beginning with the agony in Gethsemane. We see Jesus leaving the Cenacle, going down with the disciples, crossing the Kidron stream and arriving at the Garden of Olives. In that orchard there are still some very old olive trees today. Perhaps they were witnesses of what happened in their shadow that afternoon, when Christ in prayer experienced mortal anguish and "his sweat became like thick drops of blood falling to the ground" ([+[1]+]). The blood, which he had shortly before given to the Church as a drink of salvation in the Eucharistic Sacrament, began to be shed; its outpouring would later be completed on Golgotha, becoming an instrument of our redemption: "Christ as High Priest of future goods [...] entered the sanctuary once for all, not with the blood of goats or bulls, but with his own blood, achieving eternal redemption" ([+[2]+]).

When the Eucharist is celebrated before the tomb of Jesus in Jerusalem, we return in an almost tangible way to its "hour", the hour of the cross and glorification. Every priest who celebrates the Holy Mass returns spiritually to that place and at that time, together with the Christian community that participates in it.

With this encyclical Letter, I wish to arouse this Eucharistic "astonishment", in continuity with the Jubilee heritage that I wanted to leave to the Church with the Apostolic Letter Novo Millennio Ineunte and with its Marian crowning Rosarium Virginis Mariae. Contemplating the face of Christ, and contemplating him with Mary, is the "program" that I have indicated to the Church at the dawn of the third millennium, inviting it to paddle out into the deep into the waters of history with the enthusiasm of the new evangelization. The Church lives on the Eucharistic Christ, is nourished by Him and is illuminated by Him. The Eucharist is a mystery of faith and, at the same time, a "mystery of light".

When I think about the Eucharist, looking at my life as a priest, as a Bishop and as Successor of Peter, it is spontaneous for me to remember so many moments and places in which I have had the grace to celebrate it. I remember the parish church of Niegowic where I carried out my first pastoral assignment, the collegiate church of St. Florian in Krakow, the Wawel Cathedral, St. Peter's Basilica and many basilicas and churches in Rome and around the world. I have been able to celebrate Holy Mass in chapels located on mountain trails, on the shores of lakes, on the shores of the sea; I have celebrated it on altars built in stadiums, in city squares... These varied settings of my Eucharistic celebrations make me intensely experience its universal and, so to speak, cosmic character. Yes, cosmic! Because also when it is celebrated on the small altar of a church in the countryside, the Eucharist is celebrated, in a certain sense, on the altar of the world. She unites heaven and earth. It encompasses and permeates all creation. The Son of God has become man, to bring back everything created, in a supreme act of praise, to the One who made it from nothing. In this way, He, the eternal high Priest, entering the eternal sanctuary through the blood of His Cross, returns to the Creator and Father all redeemed creation. He does this through the priestly ministry of the Church and for the glory of the Holy Trinity. Truly, this is the mysterium fidei that is realized in the Eucharist: the world born from the hands of God the creator returns to Him redeemed by Christ.

How can we not admire the doctrinal exposition of the Decrees on the Most Holy Eucharist and on the Sacrosanct Sacrifice of the Mass promulgated by the Council of Trent? Those pages have guided both theology and catechesis in successive centuries, and even today they are a dogmatic reference point for the continuous renewal and growth of the People of God in faith and love for the Eucharist. 11. «The Lord Jesus, on the night in which he was betrayed» ([+[0]+]), instituted the Eucharistic Sacrifice of his body and blood. The words of the apostle Paul take us to the dramatic circumstances in which the Eucharist was born. The event of the passion and death of the Lord is indelibly inscribed in it. It not only evokes it but makes it sacramentally present. It is the sacrifice of the Cross that is perpetuated for centuries.( [+[1]+] ) This truth is well expressed by the words with which, in the Latin rite, the people respond to the priest's proclamation of the "mystery of faith": "We announce your death, Lord."

12
12. This aspect of universal charity of the Eucharistic Sacrament is founded on the Savior's own words. When instituting it, he did not limit himself to saying "This is my body", "This cup is the New Covenant in my blood", but he added "given for you... poured out for you" ([+[0]+]). He not only affirmed that what he gave them to eat and drink was his body and blood, but he also manifested his sacrificial value, making present in a sacramental way his sacrifice, which he would later fulfill on the cross a few hours later, for the salvation of all. «The mass is, at the same time and inseparably, the sacrificial memorial in which the sacrifice of the cross is perpetuated, and the sacred banquet of communion in the Body and Blood of the Lord».( [+[1]+] )

13
13. Due to its intimate relationship with the sacrifice of Golgotha, the Eucharist is a sacrifice in its own sense and not only in a generic sense, as if it were the mere offering of Christ to the faithful as spiritual food. Indeed, the gift of his love and obedience to the point of giving life ( [+[0]+] ), is first of all a gift to his Father. It is certainly a gift in favor of us, and even more so, of all humanity ([+[1]+]), but a gift above all to the Father: "sacrifice that the Father accepted, corresponding to this total donation of his Son who became "obedient until death" ([+[2]+]) with his paternal dedication, that is, with the gift of new and immortal life in the resurrection." ([+[3]+])

14
14. Christ's Easter includes, with his passion and death, also his resurrection. This is what the people's acclamation after the consecration recalls: "We proclaim your resurrection." Indeed, the Eucharistic sacrifice not only makes present the mystery of the passion and death of the Savior, but also the mystery of the resurrection, which crowns his sacrifice. As living and resurrected, Christ becomes in the Eucharist "bread of life" ([+[0]+]), "living bread" ([+[1]+]). Saint Ambrose reminded the neophytes, as an application of the event of the resurrection to their life: "If Christ is in you today, He rises for you every day." )

15
15. The sacramental representation in the Holy Mass of the sacrifice of Christ, crowned by his resurrection, implies a very special presence that – quoting the words of Paul VI – “is called “real”, not by exclusion, as if the others were not “real”, but by antonomasia, because it is substantial, since through it Christ, God and man, is certainly made present, whole and complete "( [+[0]+] ) The always valid doctrine of the Council of Trent is thus recalled: "By the consecration of the bread and wine the conversion of the entire substance of the bread into the substance of the body of Christ our Lord is accomplished, and of the entire substance of the wine into the substance of his blood. This conversion, properly and conveniently, was called transubstantiation by the holy Catholic Church. «Do not see, Saint Cyril of Jerusalem exhorts, in the bread and wine mere and natural elements, because the Lord has expressly said that they are his body and his blood: faith assures you this, even if your senses suggest otherwise. ([+[2]+] )

16
16. The saving effectiveness of the sacrifice is fully realized when communion is received by receiving the body and blood of the Lord. In itself, the Eucharistic sacrifice is oriented towards the intimate union of us, the faithful, with Christ through communion: we receive Himself, who has offered himself for us; His body, which He gave for us on the Cross; his blood, "shed for many for the forgiveness of sins" ([+[0]+]). Let us remember his words: "Just as the Father who lives sent me and I live because of the Father, so he who eats me will live because of me" ([+[1]+]). Jesus himself assures us that this union, which He relates to the Trinitarian life, is effectively realized. The Eucharist is a true banquet, in which Christ offers himself as food. When Jesus first announces this meal, his listeners are astonished and confused, forcing the Master to emphasize the objective truth of his words: "Truly, truly, I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you will have no life in you" ([+[2]+]). It is not a metaphorical food: "My flesh is true food and my blood is true drink" ([+[3]+]).

17
17. Through the communion of his body and blood, Christ also communicates his Spirit to us. Saint Ephrem writes: «He called the bread his living body, he filled it with himself and his Spirit [...], and whoever eats it with faith, eats Fire and Spirit. [...]. Take, all of you, eat of it, and eat with it the Holy Spirit. In fact, it is truly my body and whoever eats it will live eternally. ([+[0]+] )The Church asks for this divine gift, the root of all other gifts, in the Eucharistic epiclesis. It is read, for example, in the Divine Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom: "We invoke you, we pray and beseech you: send your Holy Spirit upon us all and upon these gifts [...] so that they may be purification of the soul, remission of sins and communication of the Holy Spirit to all who participate in them."([+[1]+] ) And, in the Roman Missal, the celebrant implores that: "Strengthened with the Body and Blood of your Son and filled with his Holy Spirit, let us form in Christ one body and one spirit. ([+[2]+] ) Thus, with the gift of his body and blood, Christ increases in us the gift of his Spirit, already infused in Baptism and impressed as a "seal" in the sacrament of Confirmation.

18
18. The acclamation that the people pronounce after the consecration is appropriately concluded by expressing the eschatological projection that distinguishes the Eucharistic celebration ([+[0]+]): "...until you return." The Eucharist is tension towards the goal, to taste the full joy promised by Christ ( [+[1]+] ); It is, in a certain sense, an anticipation of Paradise and a "pledge of future glory."( [+[2]+] ) In the Eucharist, everything expresses the confident expectation: "as we wait for the glorious coming of our Savior Jesus Christ."( [+[3]+] ) He who feeds on Christ in the Eucharist does not have to wait for the afterlife to receive eternal life: he already possesses it on earth as the first fruit of the future plenitude, which will encompass man in its entirety. Indeed, in the Eucharist we also receive the guarantee of bodily resurrection at the end of the world: "He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day" ([+[4]+]). This guarantee of the future resurrection comes from the fact that the flesh of the Son of Man, given as food, is his body in the glorious state of the resurrected one. With the Eucharist, the "secret" of the resurrection is assimilated, so to speak. That is why Saint Ignatius of Antioch rightly defined the Eucharistic Bread as “drug of immortality, antidote to death”.([+[5]+])

19
19. The eschatological tension raised by the Eucharist expresses and consolidates communion with the heavenly Church. It is no coincidence that in the Eastern anaphoras and in the Latin Eucharistic prayers the glorious ever Virgin Mary, Mother of Jesus Christ, our God and Lord, the angels, the holy apostles, the glorious martyrs and all the saints are always remembered with veneration. It is an aspect of the Eucharist that deserves to be highlighted: as we celebrate the sacrifice of the Lamb, we join the heavenly liturgy, associating ourselves with the immense multitude that shouts: "Salvation belongs to our God, who sits on the throne, and to the Lamb" ([+[0]+]). The Eucharist is truly a piece of heaven that opens on earth. It is a ray of glory from the heavenly Jerusalem, which penetrates the clouds of our history and sheds light on our path.

20
20. A significant consequence of the eschatological tension inherent to the Eucharist is that it gives impetus to our historical path, placing a seed of living hope in the daily dedication of each one to his own tasks. Indeed, although the Christian vision sets its sights on a "new heaven" and a "new earth" ( [+[0]+] ), this does not weaken, but rather stimulates, our sense of responsibility with respect to the present earth. ( [+[1]+] ) I wish to emphasize this strongly at the beginning of the new millennium, so that Christians feel more than ever committed to not neglecting the duties of their earthly citizenship. It is your responsibility to contribute with the light of the Gospel to the construction of a habitable world fully in accordance with God's plan.

21
21. The Second Vatican Council has recalled that the Eucharistic celebration is the center of the Church's growth process. In fact, after having said that "the Church, or the kingdom of Christ already present in mystery, grows visibly in the world by the power of God",([+[0]+]) as if wanting to answer the question: How does it grow?, he adds: "As many times as the sacrifice of the cross is celebrated on the altar, in which Christ, our Passover, was immolated ([+[1]+]), the work of our redemption is carried out. The sacrament of the Eucharistic bread signifies and at the same time realizes the unity of believers, who form a single body in Christ ( [+[2]+] ) ».( [+[3]+] )

22
22. Incorporation into Christ, which takes place through Baptism, is continually renewed and consolidated with participation in the Eucharistic Sacrifice, especially when this is complete through sacramental communion. We can say that not only does each of us receive Christ, but Christ also receives each of us. He strengthens his friendship with us: «You are my friends» ([+[0]+]). Furthermore, we live thanks to Him: "he who eats me will live because of me" ([+[1]+]). In Eucharistic communion it is realized in a sublime way that Christ and the disciple "are" in each other: "Remain in me, as I in you" ([+[2]+]).

23
23. With Eucharistic communion the Church also consolidates its unity as the body of Christ. Saint Paul refers to this unifying effectiveness of participation in the Eucharistic banquet when he writes to the Corinthians: «And the bread that we break, is it not communion with the body of Christ? For even though we are many, we are one bread and one body, for we all partake of one bread » ( [+[0]+] ). Saint John Chrysostom's commentary is detailed and profound: «What, in fact, is bread? It is the body of Christ. What are those who receive it transformed into? In the body of Christ; but not many bodies but one body. In fact, since bread is only one, even though it is composed of many grains of wheat and these are found in it, although they cannot be seen, in such a way that its diversity disappears by virtue of its perfect fusion; In the same way, we are also united reciprocally with each other and, all together, with Christ. "( [+[1]+] ) The argument is conclusive: our union with Christ, who is a gift and grace for each one, means that in Him we are also associated with the unity of his body which is the Church. The Eucharist consolidates the incorporation into Christ, established in Baptism through the gift of the Spirit ([+[2]+]).

24
24. The gift of Christ and his Spirit that we receive in Eucharistic communion more than fully fulfills the longings for fraternal unity that the human heart harbors and, at the same time, elevates the experience of fraternity, typical of common participation at the same Eucharistic table, to levels that are far above the simple human coexistence experience. Through the communion of the body of Christ, the Church reaches ever more deeply its being "in Christ as a sacrament or sign and instrument of intimate union with God and of the unity of the entire human race."( [+[0]+] )

25. The worship given to the Eucharist outside of Mass is of inestimable value in the life of the Church. This cult is closely linked to the celebration of the Eucharistic Sacrifice. The presence of Christ under the sacred species that are preserved after the Mass - a presence that lasts as long as the species of bread and wine subsist ( [+[0]+] ) - derives from the celebration of the Sacrifice and tends to sacramental and spiritual communion. the adoration of Christ present under the Eucharistic species.( [+[2]+] )

26. As I have recalled before, if the Eucharist builds the Church and the Church makes the Eucharist, it follows that there is an extremely close relationship between one and the other. This is so true that it allows us to apply to the Eucharistic Mystery what we say about the Church when, in the Nicene-Constantinopolitan Symbol, we confess it to be "one, holy, catholic and apostolic." Also the Eucharist is one and catholic. It is also holy, even more so, it is the Blessed Sacrament. But now we want to direct our attention primarily to his apostolicity.

27. The Catechism of the Catholic Church, when explaining how the Church is apostolic, that is, based on the Apostles, refers to a triple meaning of the expression. On the one hand, “it was and remains built on “the foundation of the apostles” ([+[0]+]), witnesses chosen and sent on mission by Christ himself. us. The Church celebrates the Eucharist throughout the centuries precisely in continuity with the action of the Apostles, obedient to the command of the Lord.

28
28. Finally, the Church is apostolic in the sense that "it continues to be taught, sanctified and directed by the Apostles until the return of Christ thanks to those who succeed them in their pastoral ministry: the college of Bishops, attended by the priests, together with the successor of Peter and Supreme Pastor of the Church."( [+[0]+] ) The succession of the Apostles in the pastoral mission necessarily entails the sacrament of Orders, that is, the uninterrupted series that goes back to the origins, of valid episcopal ordinations.( [+[1]+] ) This succession is essential for there to be Church in the proper and full sense.

The Eucharist celebrated by them is a gift that radically exceeds the power of the assembly and is irreplaceable in any case to validly unite the Eucharistic consecration to the sacrifice of the Cross and the Last Supper.

30. Both this doctrine of the Catholic Church on the priestly ministry in relation to the Eucharist, and that regarding the Eucharistic Sacrifice, have been the subject of a fruitful dialogue in recent decades in the field of ecumenical activity. We must give thanks to the Holy Trinity because, in this regard, significant progress and rapprochements have been obtained, which make us hope for a future in which faith is fully shared. The Council's observation on the ecclesial Communities that emerged in the West from the 16th century onwards and separated from the Catholic Church is still completely valid: "The separated ecclesial Communities, although they lack the full unity with us that comes from baptism, and although we believe that, above all due to a defect in the sacrament of Holy Orders, they have not preserved the genuine and complete substance of the Eucharistic Mystery, nevertheless, by commemorating in the Holy Supper the death and resurrection of the Lord, they profess that life is meant in the communion of Christ, and they await his glorious coming. ([+[0]+] )

31
31. If the Eucharist is the center and summit of the life of the Church, it is also the center and summit of the priestly ministry. Therefore, with a spirit of gratitude to Jesus Christ, our Lord, I reiterate that the Eucharist "is the main and central reason for being of the sacrament of the priesthood, effectively born at the moment of the institution of the Eucharist and at the same time as it."( [+[0]+] )

32. All of this demonstrates how painful and unusual the situation is for a Christian community that, although it may be, due to the number and variety of faithful, a parish, nevertheless lacks a priest to guide it. In effect, the parish is a community of baptized people who express and confirm their identity mainly through the celebration of the Eucharistic Sacrifice. But this requires the presence of a priest, the only one who is responsible for offering the Eucharist in persona Christi. When the community does not have a priest, it certainly must be alleviated in some way, so that the Sunday celebrations continue and, thus, the religious and lay people who encourage the prayer of their brothers and sisters exercise in a laudable way the common priesthood of all the faithful, based on the grace of Baptism. But these solutions must be considered only provisional, while the community is waiting for a priest.

33. When, due to a shortage of priests, participation in the pastoral care of a parish is entrusted to non-ordained faithful, they must keep in mind that, as the Second Vatican Council teaches, "no Christian community is built if it does not have as its root and center the celebration of the sacred Eucharist." have the celebration of Mass, even taking advantage of the occasional presence of a priest who is not prevented by the right of the Church from celebrating it.

34
34. In 1985, the Extraordinary Assembly of the Synod of Bishops recognized in the "ecclesiology of communion" the central and fundamental idea of the documents of the Second Vatican Council.( [+[0]+] ) The Church, while on pilgrimage here on earth, is called to maintain and promote both communion with the Trinitarian God and communion among the faithful. To do this, it relies on the Word and the Sacraments, especially the Eucharist, from which it "lives and develops without ceasing",([+[1]+]) and in which, at the same time, it expresses itself. It is no coincidence that the term communion has become one of the specific names of this sublime Sacrament.

35
35. The celebration of the Eucharist, however, cannot be the starting point of communion, which presupposes it previously, to consolidate it and bring it to perfection. The Sacrament expresses this bond of communion, whether in the invisible dimension that, in Christ and through the action of the Holy Spirit, unites us to the Father and to each other, or in the visible dimension, which implies communion in the doctrine of the Apostles, in the Sacraments and in the hierarchical order. The intimate relationship between the invisible and visible elements of the ecclesial communion is constitutive of the Church as a sacrament of salvation.( [+[0]+] ) Only in this context does the legitimate celebration of the Eucharist and true participation in it take place. Therefore, it is an intrinsic requirement of the Eucharist that it be celebrated in communion and, specifically, in the integrity of all its ties.

36
36. The invisible communion, although by nature a growth, presupposes the life of grace, through which we are made "sharers of the divine nature" ([+[0]+]), as well as the practice of the virtues of faith, hope and charity. Indeed, only in this way is true communion with the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit obtained. Faith is not enough, but it is necessary to persevere in sanctifying grace and charity, remaining within the Church with the "body" and with the "heart"; ( [+[1]+] ) that is, we need, to put it in the words of Saint Paul, "faith that acts through charity" ( [+[2]+] ).

37
37. The Eucharist and Penance are two sacraments closely linked to each other. The Eucharist, by making present the redemptive Sacrifice of the Cross, perpetuating it sacramentally, means that from it is derived a continuous demand for conversion, for a personal response to the exhortation that Saint Paul addressed to the Christians of Corinth: "In the name of Christ we beseech you: be reconciled to God! » ( [+[0]+] ). Thus, if the Christian is aware of a serious sin, he is obliged to follow the penitential itinerary, through the sacrament of Reconciliation to approach full participation in the Eucharistic Sacrifice.

38
38. Ecclesial communion, as I have recalled before, is also visible and is manifested in the binding ties enumerated by the Council itself when it teaches: "Those who, having the Spirit of Christ, fully accept its constitution and all the means of salvation established in it and are united, within its visible structure, to Christ, who governs it through the Supreme Pontiff and the Bishops, are fully incorporated into the society that is the Church. bonds of the profession of faith, of the sacraments, of ecclesiastical government and of communion. ([+[0]+] )

39
39. Furthermore, due to the very character of ecclesial communion and the relationship that the sacrament of the Eucharist has with it, it must be remembered that "the Eucharistic Sacrifice, although always celebrated in a particular community, is never a celebration of that single community: this, in fact, receiving the Eucharistic presence of the Lord, receives the complete gift of salvation, and thus manifests itself, despite its permanent visible particularity, as the image and true presence of the one Church. holy, catholic and apostolic ».( [+[0]+] ) From this it follows that a truly Eucharistic community cannot close itself in on itself, as if it were self-sufficient, but must remain in tune with all other Catholic communities.

40
40. The Eucharist creates communion and educates communion. Saint Paul wrote to the faithful of Corinth expressing the great contrast of their divisions in the Eucharistic assemblies with what they were celebrating, the Lord's Supper. Consequently, the Apostle invited them to reflect on the true reality of the Eucharist in order to make them return to the spirit of fraternal communion ( [+[0]+] ). Saint Augustine echoed this requirement eloquently when, recalling the words of the Apostle: "you are the body of Christ, and each of his members in his part" ([+[1]+]), he observed: "If you are the body and members of Christ, on the table of the Lord is the mystery that is yourselves and you receive the mystery that is you." ([+[2]+]) And, from this observation, he concluded: «Christ the Lord [...] consecrated at his table the mystery of our peace and unity. He who receives the mystery of unity and does not possess the bond of peace, does not receive a mystery for his own benefit, but rather a testimony against himself. ([+[3]+] )

41
41. This peculiar effectiveness in promoting communion, typical of the Eucharist, is one of the reasons for the importance of the Sunday Mass. I have dealt with it and the reasons why it is fundamental for the life of the Church and of each of the faithful in the Apostolic Letter on the sanctification of Sunday Dies Domini, ( [+[0]+] ) recalling, furthermore, that participating in the Mass is an obligation for the faithful, unless they have a serious impediment, which imposes on Pastors the corresponding duty to offer everyone the effective possibility of fulfilling this precept.( [+[1]+] ) More recently, in the Apostolic Letter Novo Millennio Ineunte, when tracing the pastoral path of the Church at the beginning of the third millennium, I wanted to give particular importance to the Sunday Eucharist, underlining its creative effectiveness of communion: It, I said, is the privileged place where communion is constantly announced and cultivated. Precisely through Eucharistic participation, the Lord's Day also becomes the day of the Church, which can thus effectively play its role as a sacrament of unity. "( [+[2]+] )

42
42. The safeguarding and promotion of ecclesial communion is a task of all the faithful, who find in the Eucharist, as a sacrament of the unity of the Church, a field of special application. More specifically, this task concerns with particular responsibility the Pastors of the Church, each one in their own degree and according to their own ecclesiastical office. Therefore, the Church has given norms that are aimed at promoting the frequent and fruitful participation of the faithful at the Eucharistic Table and, at the same time, at determining the objective conditions under which communion should not be administered. The effort to ensure faithful observance of these norms becomes an effective expression of love towards the Eucharist and towards the Church.

43
43. When considering the Eucharist as a Sacrament of ecclesial communion, there is an argument that, due to its importance, cannot be omitted: I am referring to its relationship with ecumenical commitment. We all have to thank the Holy Trinity that, in recent decades, many faithful in all parts of the world have felt attracted by the ardent desire for unity among all Christians. The Second Vatican Council, at the beginning of the Decree on Ecumenism, recognizes in this a special gift from God.( [+[0]+] ) It has been an effective grace, which has made both the children of the Catholic Church and our brothers from other Churches and Ecclesial Communities embark on the path of ecumenism.

44
44. Precisely because the unity of the Church, which the Eucharist brings about through sacrifice and communion in the body and blood of the Lord, irrevocably demands complete communion in the bonds of the profession of faith, the sacraments and the ecclesiastical government, it is not possible to concelebrate the same Eucharistic liturgy until the integrity of said bonds is restored. A concelebration without these conditions would not be a valid means, and could rather prove to be an obstacle to the achievement of full communion, concealing the sense of the distance that remains until reaching the goal and introducing or supporting ambiguities about one or another truth of faith. The path to full unity cannot be done if it is not in the truth. At this point, the prohibition contained in the law of the Church leaves no room for uncertainty,( [+[0]+] ) in obedience to the moral norm proclaimed by the Second Vatican Council.( [+[1]+] )

45
45. If in no case is concelebration legitimate if full communion is lacking, the same does not occur with respect to the administration of the Eucharist, in special circumstances, to people belonging to Churches or ecclesial Communities that are not in full communion with the Catholic Church. Indeed, in this case the objective is to satisfy a serious spiritual need for the eternal salvation of the faithful, considered singularly, but not to achieve an intercommunion, which is not possible until the visible links of ecclesial communion have been fully reestablished.

46
46. In the Encyclical Ut unum sint, I myself have expressed appreciation for this regulation, which allows us to attend to the salvation of souls with appropriate discernment: "It is a reason for joy to remember that Catholic ministers can, in certain particular cases, administer the sacraments of the Eucharist, of Penance, of the Anointing of the Sick to other Christians who are not in full communion with the Catholic Church, but who strongly desire to receive them, ask for them freely, and They manifest the faith that the Catholic Church confesses in these Sacraments. Reciprocally, in certain cases and due to particular circumstances, Catholics can also request the same Sacraments from the ministers of those Churches in which they are valid. ([+[0]+] )

47
47. Whoever reads the account of the Eucharistic institution in the Synoptic Gospels is impressed by the simplicity and, at the same time, the "gravity" with which Jesus, on the evening of the Last Supper, institutes the great Sacrament. There is an episode that, in a certain sense, acts as a prelude: the anointing of Bethany. A woman, whom John identifies with Mary, sister of Lazarus, pours a bottle of precious perfume on Jesus' head, provoking a reaction of protest in the disciples - particularly Judas ( [+[0]+] ) - as if this gesture were an intolerable "waste", considering the demands of the poor. But the assessment of Jesus is very different. Without taking anything away from the duty of charity towards the needy, to whom the disciples must always dedicate themselves - "you will always have the poor with you" ( [+[1]+] ) - He focuses on the imminent event of his death and burial, and appreciates the anointing that is done to him in anticipation of the honor that his body also deserves after death, for being inextricably linked to the mystery of his person.

48
48. Like the anointing woman in Bethany, the Church has not been afraid to "waste", dedicating its best resources to express its reverent amazement at the immeasurable gift of the Eucharist. No less than those first disciples in charge of preparing the "great room", the Church has felt compelled throughout the centuries and in the various cultures to celebrate the Eucharist in a context worthy of such a great Mystery. The Christian liturgy was born in continuity with the words and gestures of Jesus and developing the ritual heritage of Judaism. And, in fact, nothing will be enough to adequately express the acceptance of the gift of himself that the divine Bridegroom continually makes to the Bride Church, making the Sacrifice offered once and for all on the Cross available to all generations of believers, and becoming food for all the faithful. Although the logic of the "banquet" inspires familiarity, the Church has never given in to the temptation to trivialize this "cordiality" with her Spouse, forgetting that He is also her God and that the "banquet" always remains, after all, a sacrificial banquet, marked by the blood shed on Golgotha. The Eucharistic banquet is truly a "sacred" banquet, in which the simplicity of the signs contains the abyss of God's holiness: "O Sacrum convivium, in quo Christus sumitur! » The bread that is broken on our altars, offered to our condition as pilgrims on our way along the paths of the world, is « panis angelorum », bread of the angels, which cannot be approached except with the humility of the centurion of the Gospel: « Lord, I am not worthy that you come under my roof » ([+[0]+] ).

49
49. In the context of this elevated sense of mystery, it is understood how the Church's faith in the Eucharistic Mystery has been expressed in history not only through the demand for an interior attitude of devotion, but also through a series of external expressions, aimed at evoking and underlining the magnitude of the event being celebrated. From here comes the process that has progressively led to the establishment of a special regulation of the Eucharistic liturgy, in respect for the various legitimately constituted ecclesial traditions. Also on this basis a rich art heritage has been created. Architecture, sculpture, painting, music, allowing themselves to be guided by the Christian mystery, have found in the Eucharist, directly or indirectly, a reason for great inspiration.

50
50. In this effort to worship the Mystery, from the ritual and aesthetic point of view, the Christians of the West and the East, in a certain sense, have become mutual "competitors." How can we not thank the Lord, in particular, for the contribution that the great architectural and pictorial works of the Greco-Byzantine tradition and of the entire Slavic geographical and cultural sphere have given to Christian art? In the East, sacred art has preserved an especially intense sense of mystery, prompting artists to conceive their desire to produce beauty, not only as a manifestation of their own genius, but also as an authentic service to faith. Going far beyond mere technical skill, they have known how to open themselves with docility to the breath of the Spirit of God.

51
51. Regarding sacred art and liturgical discipline, what has occurred in lands of ancient Christianization is also happening in continents where Christianity is younger. This phenomenon has been the object of attention by the Second Vatican Council when discussing the demand for a healthy and, at the same time, obligatory "inculturation." In my numerous pastoral trips I have had the opportunity to observe in all parts of the world how much vitality the Eucharistic celebration can awaken in contact with the forms, styles and sensibilities of different cultures. Adapting to the changing conditions of time and space, the Eucharist offers nourishment, not only to people, but to the people themselves, shaping Christianly inspired cultures.

52
52. From all that has been said, we understand the great responsibility that priests have in the Eucharistic celebration, mainly, who are responsible for presiding over it in persona Christi, giving a testimony and a service of communion, not only to the community that directly participates in the celebration, but also to the universal Church, to which the Eucharist always makes reference. Unfortunately, it is to be regretted that, especially since the years of the post-conciliar liturgical reform, due to a misunderstanding sense of creativity and adaptation, there has been no shortage of abuses, which for many have been a cause of discomfort. A certain reaction to "formalism" has led some, especially in certain regions, to consider as non-obligatory the "forms" adopted by the great liturgical tradition of the Church and its Magisterium, and to introduce unauthorized and often completely inconvenient innovations.

53
53. If we want to discover in all its richness the intimate relationship that unites the Church and the Eucharist, we cannot forget Mary, Mother and model of the Church. In the Apostolic Letter Rosarium Virginis Mariae, presenting the Blessed Virgin as Teacher in the contemplation of the face of Christ, I have also included among the mysteries of light the institution of the Eucharist.( [+[0]+] ) Indeed, Mary can guide us towards this Most Holy Sacrament because she has a deep relationship with it.

54
54. Mysterium fidei! Since the Eucharist is a mystery of faith, which so surpasses our understanding that it forces us to the purest abandonment to the word of God, no one like Mary can be a support and guide in an attitude like this. Repeat the gesture of Christ at the Last Supper, in fulfillment of his command: «Do this in commemoration of me! », becomes at the same time an acceptance of Mary's invitation to obey him without hesitation: «Do whatever he tells you» ([+[0]+]). With the maternal concern that she shows at the wedding at Cana, Mary seems to tell us: "do not doubt, trust in the Word of my Son." He, who was capable of transforming water into wine, is equally capable of making bread and wine his body and blood, giving believers in this mystery the living memory of his Passover, thus becoming "bread of life."

55
55. In a certain sense, Mary has practiced her Eucharistic faith even before it was instituted, by the very fact of having offered her virginal womb for the incarnation of the Word of God. The Eucharist, while referring to the passion and resurrection, is at the same time in continuity with the Incarnation. Mary conceived the divine Son at the Annunciation, even in the physical reality of his body and blood, anticipating in herself what is carried out to a certain extent sacramentally in every believer who receives, in the species of bread and wine, the body and blood of the Lord.

56
56. Mary, with her entire life alongside Christ and not only on Calvary, made the sacrificial dimension of the Eucharist her own. When he took the child Jesus to the temple in Jerusalem "to present him to the Lord" ([+[0]+]), he heard the old Simeon announce that the child would be a "sign of contradiction" and also that a "sword" would pierce his own soul ([+[1]+]). The drama of the crucified Son was thus foreshadowed and, in a certain way, the "stabat Mater" of the Virgin at the foot of the Cross was foreshadowed. Preparing day by day for Calvary, Mary lives a kind of "anticipatory Eucharist," one could say, a "spiritual communion" of desire and offering, which will culminate in the union with the Son in the passion and will manifest itself later, in the post-Easter period, in her participation in the Eucharistic celebration, presided over by the Apostles, as a "memorial" of the passion.

57
57. «Do this in remembrance of me» ([+[0]+]). In the "memorial" of Calvary is present everything that Christ accomplished in his passion and death. Therefore, there is no lack of what Christ has also done with his Mother for our benefit. In fact, he entrusts him to his favorite disciple and, in him, gives him to each one of us: "Behold your son." Likewise he also says to all of us: «Here is your mother! » ( [+[1]+] ).

58
58. In the Eucharist, the Church fully unites itself with Christ and his sacrifice, making the spirit of Mary her own. It is a truth that can be deepened by rereading the Magnificat from a Eucharistic perspective. The Eucharist, in fact, like Mary's song, is above all praise and thanksgiving. When Mary exclaims "my soul magnifies the Lord, my spirit exults in God, my Savior", she carries Jesus in her womb. He praises the Father “through” Jesus, but he also praises Him “in” Jesus and “with” Jesus. This is precisely the true "Eucharistic attitude."

59
59. « Ave, verum corpus natum of Maria Virgine! ». A few years ago I celebrated the fiftieth anniversary of my priesthood. Today I experience the grace of offering this Encyclical on the Eucharist to the Church, on Holy Thursday of my twenty-fifth year of Petrine ministry. I do it with a heart full of gratitude. For more than half a century, every day, since November 2, 1946, when I celebrated my first Mass in the crypt of Saint Leonard of the Wawel Cathedral in Krakow, my eyes have been fixed on the host and the chalice in which, in a certain way, time and space have been "concentrated" and the drama of Golgotha ​​has been represented in a living way, revealing its mysterious "contemporaneity." Every day, my faith has been able to recognize in the consecrated bread and wine the divine Wayfarer who one day stood next to the two disciples of Emmaus to open their eyes to light and their hearts to hope ( [+[0]+] ).

60
60. At the dawn of this third millennium all of us, children of the Church, are called to walk in the Christian life with renewed momentum. As I wrote in the Apostolic Letter Novo Millennio Ineunte, it is not a question of "inventing a new program." The program already exists. It is the same as always, collected by the Gospel and living Tradition. It focuses, ultimately, on Christ himself, whom we must know, love and imitate, to live in him the Trinitarian life and with him transform history until its perfection in the heavenly Jerusalem.

61
61. The Eucharistic Mystery – sacrifice, presence, banquet – does not allow reductions or instrumentalizations; It must be lived in its entirety, whether during the celebration, whether in the intimate conversation with Jesus as soon as received in communion, or during Eucharistic adoration outside of Mass. That is when the Church is firmly built and truly expresses what it is: one, holy, catholic and apostolic; people, temple and family of God; body and bride of Christ, animated by the Holy Spirit; universal sacrament of salvation and hierarchically structured communion.

62
62. Let us follow, dear brothers and sisters, the teaching of the Saints, great interpreters of true Eucharistic piety. With them the theology of the Eucharist acquires all the splendor of the lived experience, it "infects" us and, so to speak, "ignites" us. Let us, above all, listen to the Most Holy Mary, in whom the Eucharistic Mystery is shown, more than in any other, as a mystery of light. By looking at her we know the transformative force that the Eucharist has. In it we see the world renewed by love. When we contemplate her assumed into heaven in soul and body we see a glimpse of the "new heaven" and the "new earth" that will open before our eyes with the second coming of Christ. The Eucharist is already here, on earth, its pledge and, in a certain way, its anticipation: « Come, Domine Iesu! » ( [+[0]+] ).
