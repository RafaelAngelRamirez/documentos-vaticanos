1
Canon 1 In everything the ancient customs which have hitherto obtained in the holy churches of God must be observed. And if in any case something has been omitted through ignorance or necessity, the rest should be diligently observed.

2
Canon 2 The holy and ecumenical council of Nicaea, and the four ecumenical councils that after it, and those that thereafter met in this royal city, and their canons and definitions, we firmly hold and observe.

3
Canon 3 We define that no layman or monk or clergyman who strays from his church will be received into another church without letters from his bishop. And whoever does otherwise, both the one who received and the one received, are subject to canonical penalties.

4
Canon 4 Photius, who by violence and tyranny invaded the throne of Constantinople, and all the things that were done disorderly through him, we condemn and anathematize, and decree that he is alien to the priestly degree.

5
Canon 5 Those who were ordained disorderly by Photius, unless by penance and canonical reception they return to Catholic unity, we command that they be alien to the clergy.

6
Canon 6 That no one be promoted to the episcopate without the consensus of the elected and the clergy and the people, and without the examination of the metropolitan and the provincial bishops. Whoever is otherwise ordained, let his ordination be void.

7
Canon 7 That bishops should not get involved in secular business, but rather dedicate themselves to prayer and the word of God, according to apostolic tradition.

8
Canon 8 If any bishop or clergyman acquires ordination for money, both the one who gave and the one who received must be demoted from the rank, and the money must be restored to the churches.

9
Canon 9 Let monasteries that have once been consecrated to God remain monasteries forever, and do not become secular habitations.

10
Canon 10 It is not lawful for the faithful to read or retain the books of heretics and schismatics, and especially those that have been written against sound doctrine; but that they be burned or handed over to the bishops.

11
Canon 11 We receive and venerate the images of our Lord Jesus Christ and of the holy Mother of God and of the saints, according to the definition of the second synod of Nicaea.

12
Canon 12 It is not lawful for clerics or monks to undertake long pilgrimages without permission from their prelate, so as not to cause scandal and dissolve discipline.

13
Canon 13 We command that provincial synods be held at least once a year, so that ecclesiastical affairs may be examined and corrected.

14
Canon 14 If someone justly suspended from ecclesiastical communion takes refuge in secular judgments, and not in the superior ecclesiastical judge: let him be suspended for a longer time, until he renders due obedience.

15
Canon 15 It is appropriate that in each church bursars be established, who faithfully administer ecclesiastical matters with the bishop and render an account.

16
Canon 16 We entrust the widows and the wards and the poor especially to the care of the bishops, so that they may be helped as much as possible by the ecclesiastical faculties.

17
Canon 17 That bigamous clerics, or those who took a widow as a wife, or those who after baptism went to war in a disorderly manner, should not be admitted to sacred orders.

18
Canon 18 If any bishop is absent from his see for a long time without reasonable cause, and neglects the flock: let him be corrected by the provincial synod; and if he does not mend his ways, let him be deposed.

19
Canon 19 That schismatics and heretics are in no way received into communion, unless they return to the unity of the Catholic Church.

20
Canon 20 All these things, piously and canonically constituted by us, we command that they be firmly observed by all. And whoever despises them must be subject to canonical penalties, so that peace and order may be preserved in the holy Church of God.
