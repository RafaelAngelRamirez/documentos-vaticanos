1
Definition of faith about the two wills

2
The holy and great and ecumenical synod, assembled in this royal city preserved by God, according to the disposition of God and according to the pious decree of our most pious and most faithful great emperor Constantine, in the great and preserved basilica of God called Hagia Sophia: following the five holy and ecumenical synods and the holy and proven Fathers, and defining one mouth and one soul, confesses our Lord Jesus Christ, true our God, one of the holy and consubstantial and life-giving Trinity, perfect in deity and perfect in humanity, true God and true man the same, of rational soul and body, consubstantial with the Father according to the deity and consubstantial with us the same according to humanity, similar to us in everything except sin; begotten, in truth, before the ages of the Father according to the deity, and in the last days the same for us and for our salvation of the Holy Spirit and of the virgin Mary, who is properly and truly Mother of God, according to humanity; one and the same Christ the Son, the only begotten Lord, who is to be recognized in two natures without confusion, without change, without separation, without division; nowhere removed the difference of natures because of the union, but rather saved the property of both natures and concurring in a single person and a single subsistence; not divided nor divided into two persons, but one and the same only begotten Son God the Word Lord Jesus Christ; as from the beginning the prophets about him and Jesus Christ himself instructed us and the symbol of the holy Fathers transmitted to us.

3
And we also preach, according to the doctrine of the holy Fathers, two natural wills in him and two natural operations, without division, without conversion, without separation, without confusion; and the two natural wills are not contrary, absit, as the impious heretics said, but follows his human will, and does not resist or oppose, but rather is subject to his divine and omnipotent will. It was appropriate, in fact, that the will of the flesh should move, but be subject to the divine will, according to the most wise Athanasius. For just as his flesh is said to be and is the flesh of the Word God, so also the natural will of his flesh is said to be and is proper to the Word God, as he himself says: "For I have come down from heaven, not to do my will, but the will of him who sent me, the Father." Calling the will of the flesh his own: since the flesh also became his own. For just as his most holy and immaculate deified animated flesh was not suppressed, but remained in its own state and reason: so also his deified human will was not suppressed, but rather was saved, as the divine Gregory says: "For the will of the one, which is understood in the Savior, is not contrary to God, all deified."

4
And we glorify two natural operations without division, without conversion, without confusion, without separation in the same our Lord Jesus Christ our true God: that is, the divine operation and the human operation, as the divine Leo most luculently affirms: "For each of the two forms works, in communion with the other, what is proper to it: the Word, namely, operating what is of the Word, and the flesh executing what is of the flesh." Because in no way will we confess a single and same natural operation of God and the creature, so as not to elevate what is created to the divine essence, nor to lower the exalt of the divine nature to the place of creatures. For of one and the same we know, of the diverse natures, the miracles and the passions, of which and in which and which it is, operating inseparably.

5
And these two wills and natural operations, in indissoluble union, not confusing them, not converting them, not separating them, not dividing them, and in no way exterminating them, but rather we confess the essential difference of the natures; and remaining saved in the union, and we preach only one and the same operating in both and of both, and one and the same Lord our and true God Jesus Christ: not only of the deity, but also of humanity, without sin.

6
Canon 6 If anyone does not confess our Lord Jesus Christ, true God and true man, one of the holy Trinity, in two natures and two wills and two operations: let him be anathema.

7
Canon 7 If anyone defends the impious Monothelites and Monenergists, and their criminal dogmas: let him be anathema.

8
Canon 8 If anyone does not anathematize the impious Sergius, Cyrus, Pyrrhus, Paul, Peter, and their like, and Honorius who favored their heresy, and their impious writings: let him be anathema.
