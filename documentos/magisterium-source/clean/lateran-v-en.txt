Canon 1 We firmly hold the Catholic faith in the immortality of the rational soul, and in its multiplication according to the multitude of bodies.

2
Canon 2 Books and doctrines adverse to the faith must not be spread among the people without due ecclesiastical censorship.

8
Sessio 8 de anima With the approval of the present sacred council, we condemn and reprove all those who affirm that the intellective soul is mortal, or unique in all men, and those who doubt this: since it not only truly exists by itself and essentially as a form of the human body, as contained in the canon of Pope Clement V, of happy memory, our predecessor, published at the council of Vienne, but it is also immortal, and singularly multiplyable, and multiplied, and by multiplying, according to the multitude of bodies into which it is infused.

9
Session 9 We establish and order that in the public lessons of all general studies, where the nature of the soul and things that concern faith are discussed, the truth of the Catholic faith be openly taught, and opinions contrary to the faith be refuted and confuted, as far as possible.

10
Session 10 of impressione librorum Since the printing and multiplication of books, if not governed correctly, can tend to the perdition of faith and customs: we establish that from now on no one dares to print books on things of faith and customs, unless they have first been examined and approved by the ordinary of the place, or by another delegated by him. Those who act otherwise are subject to censorship and penalties at the discretion of the ordinary, and printed books are burned.

11
Session 11 of pace As far as possible, peace and concord be fostered between Christian princes and peoples, so that forces may be turned against the infidels and for the reform of the Church, and scandals may be eliminated.

7
Canon 7 Tithes and ecclesiastical rights must not be usurped by the laity.
