1
1. When preparing to deal with the place occupied by the Blessed Virgin in Christian worship, we must first direct our attention to the sacred Liturgy; In fact, in addition to its rich doctrinal content, it has incomparable pastoral effectiveness and a recognized example value for other forms of worship. We would have liked to take into consideration the different Liturgies of the East and the West; but, taking into account the purpose of this document, we will focus almost exclusively on the books of the Roman Rite: in fact, only this has been the object, according to the practical norms given by the Second Vatican Council ([+[0]+]), of a profound renewal, even with regard to the expressions of veneration of Mary and which requires, therefore, to be carefully considered and valued.

2
2. The reform of the Roman Liturgy presupposed a careful review of its General Calendar. This, ordered to give due emphasis to the celebration of the work of salvation on specific days, distributing throughout the annual cycle the entire mystery of Christ, from the Incarnation to the expectation of his glorious coming ([+[0]+]), has allowed the memory of the Mother to be included in a more organic way and with closer cohesion within the annual cycle of the mysteries of the Son.

3
3. Thus, during the season of Advent the Liturgy frequently remembers the Blessed Virgin – apart from the solemnity of December 8, in which the Immaculate Conception of Mary, the radical preparation ( [+[0]+] ) for the coming of the Savior and the happy exordium of the Church without spot or wrinkle ( [+[1]+] ) are celebrated – on all the holiday days from the 17th to the December 24 and, more specifically, the Sunday before Christmas, when ancient prophetic voices about the Virgin Mother and the Messiah resonate ( [+[2]+] ), and evangelical episodes relating to the imminent birth of Christ and the Precursor are read ( [+[3]+] ).

4
4. In this way, the faithful who live the spirit of Advent with the Liturgy, when considering the ineffable love with which the Virgin Mother awaited the Son ( [+[0]+] ), will feel encouraged to take her as a model and to prepare, "vigilant in prayer and... jubilant in praise" ( [+[1]+] ), to go out to meet the Savior who comes. We also want to observe how in the Liturgy of Advent, uniting the messianic expectation and the expectation of the glorious return of Christ to the admirable memory of the Mother, it presents a happy cultural balance, which can be taken as a norm to prevent any tendency to separate, as has sometimes happened in some forms of popular piety, the cult of the Virgin from its necessary point of reference: Christ. It thus turns out that this period, as specialists in liturgy have observed, must be considered a time particularly suitable for the cult of the Mother of the Lord: an orientation that we confirm and wish to see welcomed and followed everywhere.

5
5. The Christmas season constitutes a prolonged memory of the divine, virginal, salvific motherhood of Her "whose intact virginity gave this world a Savior" ( [+[0]+] ): indeed, on the solemnity of the Nativity of the Lord, the Church, by worshiping the divine Savior, venerates her glorious Mother: on the Epiphany of the Lord, by celebrating the universal call to salvation, she contemplates the Virgin, true Seat of Wisdom and true Mother of the King, who offers the Redeemer of all nations to the adoration of the Magi ( [+[1]+] ); and on the feast of the Holy Family ( [+[2]+] ), he reverently scrutinizes the holy life led by the house of Nazareth Jesus, Son of God and Son of Man, Mary, his Mother, and Joseph, the just man ( [+[3]+] ).

6
6. To the two solemnities already mentioned – the Immaculate Conception and the Divine Motherhood – must be added the ancient and venerable celebrations of March 25 and August 15.

7
7. After these solemnities, we must consider, above all, the celebrations that commemorate salvific events, in which the Virgin was closely linked to the Son, such as the festivities of the Nativity of Mary ( [+[0]+] ), "hope of the whole world and dawn of salvation" ( [+[1]+] ); of the Visitation ( [+[2]+] ), in which the Liturgy remembers the "Blessed Virgin... who carries the Son in her womb" ( [+[3]+] ), who approaches Elizabeth to offer her the help of her charity and proclaim the mercy of God the Savior ( [+[4]+] ); or also the memory of the Sorrowful Virgin ([+[5]+]), a propitious occasion to relive a decisive moment in the history of salvation and to venerate together with the Son "exalted on the Cross the Mother who shares her pain" ([+[6]+]).

8
8. Although the restored Roman Calendar highlights above all the celebrations mentioned above, it nevertheless includes other types of memories or festivals linked to local cult reasons, but which have acquired a broader interest ( [+[0]+] ); to others originally celebrated in certain religious families, but which today, due to the diffusion achieved, can be considered truly ecclesial ( [+[1]+] ); and some more that, regardless of the apocryphal aspect, propose contents of high exemplary value, continuing venerable traditions, rooted above all in the East ( [+[2]+] ); or they manifest orientations that spring from contemporary piety ( [+[3]+] ).

9
9. Nor should it be forgotten that the General Roman Calendar does not record all the celebrations with Marian content: it is up to the particular Calendars to collect, with fidelity to the liturgical norms but also with heartfelt adherence, the Marian feasts of the different local Churches. And we need to mention the possibility of a frequent Marian liturgical commemoration with recourse to the Memory of Saint Mary "on Sabbath": ancient and discreet memory, which the flexibility of the current Calendar and the multiplicity of the Missal forms make extraordinarily easy and varied.

10
10. In this Apostolic Exhortation we do not attempt to consider the entire content of the new Roman Missal, but, in order to the work of evaluation that we have planned to carry out in relation to the restored books of the Roman Rite ([+[0]+]), we wish to highlight some aspects and themes. And we want, above all, to highlight how the Eucharistic prayers of the Missal, in admirable convergence with the Eastern liturgies ([+[1]+]), contain a significant memory of the Blessed Virgin. This is what the ancient Roman Canon does, which commemorates the Mother of the Lord in dense terms of doctrine and cultural inspiration: "In communion with the entire Church, we venerate the memory, above all, of the glorious ever Virgin Mary, Mother of Jesus Christ, our God and Lord"; so also the recent Canon III, which expresses with intense longing the desire of those praying to share with the Mother the inheritance of children: "May He transform us into a permanent offering, so that we may enjoy your inheritance together with your chosen ones: with Mary, the Virgin." This daily memory, due to its placement at the center of the Holy Sacrifice, must be considered a particularly expressive form of the worship that the Church renders to the "Blessed of the Most High" ( [+[2]+] ).

11
11. Going later through the texts of the restored Missal, we see how the great Marian themes of Roman eulogy - the theme of the Immaculate Conception and the fullness of grace, of divine Motherhood, of the most complete and fruitful virginity, of the "temple of the Holy Spirit", of cooperation in the work of the Son, of exemplary holiness, of merciful intercession, of the Assumption to heaven, of maternal royalty and some moreâ€”have been collected in perfect continuity with the past, and how other themes, new in a certain sense, have been introduced in perfect adherence to the theological development of our time. Thus, for example, the Mary-Church theme has been introduced in the texts of the Missal with a variety of aspects such as the varied and multiple relationships that mediate between the Mother of Christ and the Church. Indeed, these texts, in the spotless Conception of the Virgin, recognize the exordium of the Church, the spotless Wife of Christ ( [+[0]+] ); In the Assumption they recognize the principle already fulfilled and the image of that which, for the entire Church, must still be fulfilled ([+[1]+]); in the mystery of Motherhood they proclaim her Mother of the Head and of the members: Holy Mother of God, then, and prolific Mother of the Church ( [+[2]+] ).

12
12. The Lectionary of the Mass is one of the books of the Roman Rite that has benefited more than the texts included, either because of its intrinsic value: it is, in fact, texts that contain the word of God, always living and effective ( [+[0]+] ). This abundant selection of biblical texts has made it possible to expose the entire history of salvation in an orderly three-year cycle and to propose the mystery of Christ more fully. As a logical consequence, it has resulted that the Lectionary contains a greater number of Old Testament and New Testament readings related to the blessed Virgin, a numerical increase not lacking, however, in a serene criticism, because only those readings have been collected that, either by the evidence of their content or by the indications of a careful exegesis, supported by the teachings of the Magisterium or by a solid tradition, can be considered, although in different ways and to different degrees, of Marian character. Furthermore, it should be noted that these readings are not exclusively limited to the feasts of the Virgin, but are proclaimed on many other occasions: on some Sundays of the liturgical year ([+[1]+]), in the celebration of rites that deeply touch the sacramental life of the Christian and his choices ([+[2]+]), as well as in happy or sad circumstances of his existence ([+[3]+]).

13
13. The restored book of The Liturgy of the Hours also contains illustrious testimonies of piety towards the Mother of the Lord: in the hymnic compositions, among which there are no shortage of works of art from universal literature, such as Dante's sublime prayer to the Virgin ( [+[0]+] ); in the antiphons that close the Divine Office of each day, lyrical implorations, to which has been added the famous tropary "Sub tuum praesidium", venerable for its antiquity and admirable for its content; in the intercessions of Lauds and Vespers, in which confident recourse to the Mother of Mercy is not uncommon; in the vast selection of Marian pages due to authors from the first centuries of Christianity, the Middle Ages and the modern age.

14
14. If in the Missal, in the Lectionary and in the Liturgy of the Hours, cornerstones of Roman liturgical prayer, the memory of the Virgin returns with frequent rhythm, the other restored liturgical books also lack expressions of love and supplicating veneration towards the "Theotocos": thus the Church invokes her as Mother of grace before the immersion of the candidates in the regenerating waters of baptism ([+[0]+]); implores her intercession on the mothers who, grateful for the gift of motherhood, joyfully appear in the temple ( [+[1]+] ); He offers her as an example to his members who embrace the emergence of Christ in religious life ([+[2]+]) or receive virginal consecration ([+[3]+]), and asks for her maternal help ([+[4]+]); a She addresses insistent supplications in favor of the children who have arrived at the time of transit ( [+[5]+] ); asks for his intercession for those who, having closed their eyes to the temporal light, have presented themselves before Christ, eternal Light ( [+[6]+] ); and invokes, through her intercession, consolation for those who, immersed in pain, mourn with faith separation from their loved ones ( [+[7]+] ).

15
15. The examination carried out on the restored liturgical books leads, therefore, to a comforting observation: the post-conciliar establishment, as was already in the spirit of the Liturgical Movement, has considered the Virgin in the mystery of Christ as an adequate perspective and, in harmony with tradition, has recognized her the unique position that corresponds to her within the Christian cult, as the Holy Mother of God, closely associated with the Redeemer.

16
16. Missale Romanum, Prex Eucharistica I, Communicantes in Nativitate Domini et per octavem.

17
17. Mary is the "hearing Virgin", who accepts the word of God with faith: faith, which for her was the premise and path towards divine Motherhood, because, as St. Augustine intuited: "the blessed Virgin Mary conceived believing the ( [+[0]+] ) who gave birth believing" ( [+[1]+] ); in fact, when she received the answer to her doubt from the Angel ( [+[2]+] ) "She, full of faith, and conceiving Christ in her mind rather than in her womb," said: "behold, I am the handmaid of the Lord, let it be done to me according to your word" ( [+[3]+] ) ( [+[4]+] ); faith, which was for her the cause of blessedness and security in the fulfillment of the word of the Lord" ([+[5]+]): faith, with which She, protagonist and singular witness of the Incarnation, returned to the events of Christ's childhood, confronting them with each other in the depths of her heart ([+[6]+]). This is what the Church does, which, above all in the sacred Liturgy, listens with faith, welcomes, proclaims, venerates the word of God, distributes it to the faithful as the bread of life ([+[7]+]) and searches in its light the signs of the times, interprets and lives the events of history.

18
18. Mary is also the "praying Virgin." This is how She appears in the visit to the Mother of the Precursor, where she opens her spirit in expressions of glorification of God, of humility, of faith, of hope: such is the "Magnificat" ([+[0]+]), the prayer par excellence of Mary, the song of messianic times, in which the exultation of the old and the new Israel come together, because - as St. Irenaeus seems to suggest - in Mary's song Abraham's joy flowed as he foresaw the Messiah ( [+[1]+] ) ( [+[2]+] ) and the voice of the Church resounded, prophetically anticipated: "Leaping for joy, Mary prophetically proclaims the name of the Church: "My soul magnifies the Lord..." ( [+[3]+] ). In fact, the song of the Virgin, as it spread, has become a prayer of the entire Church at all times.

19
19. Mary is also the "Virgin-Mother", that is, the one who "by her faith and obedience engendered on earth the same Son of the Father, without contact with man, but covered by the shadow of the Holy Spirit" ([+[0]+]): prodigious motherhood constituted by God as a "type" and "exemplary" of the fertility of the Virgin-Church, who "becomes herself a Mother, because with the preaching and "baptism begets to a new and immortal life children, conceived by the Holy Spirit, and born of God" ([+[1]+]). Precisely the ancient Fathers taught that the Church prolongs the virginal Motherhood of Mary in the sacrament of Baptism. Among his testimonies we are pleased to remember that of our distinguished Predecessor Saint Leo the Great, who in a natal homily affirms: "The origin that ( [+[2]+] ) took in the womb of the Virgin, he has placed in the baptismal font: he has given to water what he gave to the Mother; in fact, the virtue of the Most High and the shadow of the Holy Spirit ( [+[3]+] ), which made Mary give birth to the Savior, also makes May the water regenerate the believer" ( [+[4]+] ). Wanting to drink ( [+[5]+] ), a relative mystery of salvation in liturgical sources, we could quote the Illatio of the Hispanic liturgy: "She ( [+[6]+] ) carried Life in her womb, the latter ( [+[7]+] ) in baptism. In the members of the former Christ was embodied, in the baptismal waters the regenerate puts on Christ" ( [+[8]+] ).

20
20. Finally, Mary is the "offering Virgin." In the episode of the Presentation of Jesus in the Temple ( [+[0]+] ), the Church, guided by the Spirit, has glimpsed, beyond the fulfillment of the laws relating to the oblation of the firstborn ( [+[1]+] ) and the purification of the mother ( [+[2]+] ), a mystery of salvation related to salvific history: that is, it has noted the continuity of the fundamental offer that the incarnate Word made to the Father upon entering into the world ( [+[3]+] ); has seen the universality of salvation proclaimed, because Simeon, greeting in the Child the light that illuminates the people and the glory of Israel ( [+[4]+] ), recognized in Him the Messiah, the Savior of all; has understood the prophetic reference to the passion of Christ: that the words of Simeon, which united in a single prediction the Son, "a sign of contradiction", ([+[5]+]), and the Mother, whose soul was to be pierced by the sword ([+[6]+]), were fulfilled on Calvary. Mystery of salvation, then, that the episode of the Presentation in the Temple orients in its various aspects towards the salvific event of the cross. But the Church itself, especially since the centuries of the Middle Ages, has perceived in the heart of the Virgin who takes the Child to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord ([+[7]+]), a will to oblate that transcended the ordinary meaning of the rite. Of this intuition we find a testimony in the affectionate apostrophe of St. Bernard: "Offer your Son, sacred Virgin, and present to the Lord the blessed fruit of your womb. Offer for the reconciliation of all of us the holy victim, pleasing to God" ( [+[8]+] ).

21
21. An example for the entire Church in the exercise of divine worship, Mary is also, evidently, a teacher of spiritual life for each of the Christians. Very soon the faithful began to focus on Mary to, like Her, make their own life a cult of God, and their worship a commitment to life. Already in the 4th century, St. Ambrose, speaking to the faithful, made wishes that the soul of Mary would be in each one of them to glorify God: "That the soul of Mary is in each one to praise the Lord; that her spirit is in each one so that he may rejoice in God" ([+[0]+]). But Mary is, above all, a model of that cult that consists of making one's life an offering to God: ancient, perennial doctrine, which each one can listen to again by paying attention to the teaching of the Church, but also with an attentive ear to the voice of the Virgin when She, anticipating in herself the stupendous request of the Sunday prayer "Thy will be done" ([+[1]+]), responded to the messenger of God: "Behold the handmaid of the Lord, let it be done." in me according to your word" ( [+[2]+] ). And Mary's "yes" is for all Christians a lesson and an example to convert obedience to the will of the Father, a path and a means of self-sanctification.

22
22. On the other hand, it is important to observe how the Church translates the multiple relationships that unite her to Mary in different and effective cultural attitudes: in profound veneration, when she reflects on the singular dignity of the Virgin, converted, by the work of the Holy Spirit, into Mother of the Incarnate Word; in ardent love, when considering the spiritual Motherhood of Mary towards all the members of the mystical Body; in confident invocation, when she experiences the intercession of her Advocate and Helper ([+[0]+]); in service of love, when he discovers in the humble servant of the Lord the Queen of mercy and the Mother of grace; in operatic imitation, when he contemplates the holiness and virtues of the "full of grace" ( [+[1]+] ); in moved amazement, when he contemplates in Her, "as in a most pure image, everything she desires and hopes to be" ( [+[2]+] ); in attentive study, when she recognizes in the Cooperator of the Redeemer, already fully participating in the fruits of the Paschal Mystery, the prophetic fulfillment of her own future, until the day when, purified of every wrinkle and every stain ([+[3]+]), she will become a bride adorned for the Bridegroom Jesus Christ ([+[4]+]).

23
23. Considering, therefore, venerable brothers, the veneration that the liturgical tradition of the universal Church and the renewed Roman Rite manifest towards the holy Mother of God; remembering that the Liturgy, due to its preeminent cultural value, constitutes a golden standard for Christian piety; By observing, finally, how the Church, when celebrating the sacred mysteries, adopts an attitude of faith and love similar to those of the Virgin, we understand how just the exhortation of the Second Vatican Council to all the children of the Church "to generously promote the cult, especially liturgical, of the blessed Virgin" ([+[0]+]); exhortation that we would like to see unreservedly welcomed everywhere and zealously put into practice.

24
24. But the Second Vatican Council itself exhorts to promote, along with liturgical worship, other forms of piety, especially those recommended by the Magisterium ( [+[0]+] ). However, as is well known, the veneration of the faithful towards the Mother of God has taken different forms depending on the circumstances of place and time, the different sensibilities of the people and their different cultural traditions. Thus it turns out that the forms in which this piety is manifested, subject to the wear and tear of time, seem to need a renewal that allows obsolete elements to be replaced, give value to perennial ones and incorporate new doctrinal data acquired by theological reflection and proposed by the ecclesiastical teaching. This shows the need for Episcopal Conferences, local Churches, religious families and communities of faithful to encourage genuine creative activity and, at the same time, proceed with a diligent review of the exercises of piety to the Virgin; The review we wanted was respectful of healthy tradition and open to reflecting the legitimate aspirations of the men of our time. Therefore, it seems appropriate to us, venerable brothers, to indicate to you some principles that serve as a basis for work in this field.

25
25. First of all, it is extremely convenient that the exercises of piety to the Virgin Mary clearly express the Trinitarian and Christological note that is intrinsic and essential to them. Indeed, Christian worship is by its nature worship of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit or, as it is said in the Liturgy, the Father through Christ in the Spirit. In this perspective it legitimately extends, although in an essentially different way, first and in a singular way to the Mother of the Lord and then to the Saints, in whom the Church proclaims the Paschal Mystery, because they have suffered with Christ and with Him they have been glorified ([+[0]+]). In the Virgin Mary everything is referred to Christ and everything depends on Him: in view of Him, God the Father chose her from all eternity as an all-holy Mother and adorned her with gifts of the Holy Spirit that were not granted to any other. Certainly, genuine Christian piety has never failed to highlight the indissoluble bond and essential reference of the Virgin to the Divine Savior ( [+[1]+] ). However, it seems to us to be particularly in accordance with the spiritual trends of our time, dominated and absorbed by the "question of Christ" ( [+[2]+] ), that in the expressions of worship of the Virgin the Christological aspect is placed in particular emphasis and is done in such a way that they reflect the plan of God, who pre-established "with a single and same decree the origin of Mary and the incarnation of divine Wisdom" ( [+[3]+] ). This will undoubtedly contribute to making piety towards the Mother of Jesus more solid and to making that same piety an effective instrument to reach "the full knowledge of the Son of God, until reaching the measure of the fullness of Christ" ( [+[4]+] ); On the other hand, it will contribute to increasing the worship due to Christ himself because, according to the perennial sentiment of the Church, confirmed in an authoritative manner in our days ( [+[5]+] ), "what is offered as service to the Handmaid is attributed to the Lord; in this way what is due to the Mother is in favor of the Son; and thus the honor rendered as a humble tribute to the Queen falls equally on the King" ( [+[6]+] ).

26
26. To this allusion to the Christological orientation of the cult of the Virgin, we find it useful to add a call for the opportunity to give adequate emphasis to one of the essential contents of faith: the Person and work of the Holy Spirit. Theological reflection and the Liturgy have highlighted, in effect, how the sanctifying intervention of the Spirit in the Virgin of Nazareth has been a culminating moment of its action in the history of salvation. Thus, for example, some Holy Fathers and ecclesiastical writers attributed the original holiness of Mary to the action of the Spirit, "as shaped and converted into a new creature" by Him ( [+[0]+] ); reflecting on the evangelical texts - "the Holy Spirit will descend upon you and the power of the Most High will overshadow you" ( [+[1]+] ) and "Mary... was found pregnant by the work of the Holy Spirit; ( [+[2]+] ) it is the work of the Holy Spirit that what has been engendered in Her" ( [+[3]+] ) -, they discovered in the intervention of the Holy Spirit an action that He consecrated and made Mary's virginity fruitful ([+[4]+]) and transformed it into the Hall of the King ([+[5]+]), Temple or Tabernacle of the Lord ([+[6]+]), Ark of the Covenant or Sanctification ([+[7]+]); titles all of them rich in biblical resonances; delving deeper into the mystery of the Incarnation, they saw in the mysterious Spirit-Mary relationship a nuptial aspect, described poetically by Prudentius: "the nubile Virgin espouses the Spirit ([+[8]+]), and they called her the tabernacle of the Holy Spirit ([+[9]+]), an expression that underlines the sacred character of the Virgin converted into a stable mansion of the Spirit of God; delving into the doctrine about the Paraclete, they saw that from Him flowed, as from a spring, the fullness of grace ( [+[10]+] ) and the abundance of gifts that adorned it: hence they attributed to the Spirit the faith, hope and charity that animated the heart of the Virgin, the strength that sustained her adherence to the will of God, the vigor that sustained her during her "compassion" at the foot of the cross ( [+[11]+] ); [+[12]+] ) a particular influence of that Spirit who had spoken through the mouth of the prophets ( [+[13]+] ); finally, considering the presence of the Mother of Jesus in the Upper Room, where the Spirit descended on the nascent Church ( [+[14]+] ), they enriched the old Mary-Church theme with new data ( [+[15]+] ); the ability to engender Christ in his own soul, as St. Ildefonso attests in a prayer, surprising for his doctrine and his supplicating vigor: "I ask you, I ask you, O Holy Virgin, to obtain Jesus through the mediation of the same Spirit, by which you have engendered Jesus. May my soul receive Jesus by the work of the Spirit, by whom your flesh has conceived Jesus himself ( [+[16]+] ). May I love Jesus in the same Spirit, in which you worship him as Lord and contemplate him as Son" ( [+[17]+] ).

27
27. It is frequently stated that many texts of modern piety do not sufficiently reflect the entire doctrine about the Holy Spirit. It is the studies that have to verify this statement and measure its reach; a It is up to us to exhort everyone, especially pastors and theologians, to deepen their reflection on the action of the Holy Spirit in the history of salvation and ensure that the texts of Christian piety make its life-giving action duly clear; From such reflection will appear, in particular, the mysterious relationship between the Spirit of God and the Virgin of Nazareth, as well as its action on the Church; In this way, the more deeply measured content of faith will give rise to a more intensely lived piety.

28
28. It is also necessary that the exercises of piety, through which the faithful express their veneration of the Mother of the Lord, make more clearly evident the position that she occupies in the Church: "the highest and closest to us after Christ" ( [+[0]+] ); a position that in the buildings of worship of the Byzantine Rite have their plastic expression in the same arrangement of the architectural parts and iconographic elements - in the central door of the iconostasis the Annunciation of Mary is figured in the apse of the representation of the glorious "Theotocos" - in order to make it clear how from the "fiat" of the humble Handmaid of the Lord, humanity begins its return to God and how in the glory of the "All Beautiful" he discovers the goal of his path. The symbolism through which the Church building expresses the place of Mary in the mystery of the Church contains a fruitful indication and constitutes an auspice so that everywhere the different ways of venerating the blessed Virgin Mary are opened to ecclesial perspectives.

29
29. To the previous indications, which arise from considering the relationships of the Virgin Mary with God "Father, Son and Holy Spirit" and with the Church, we want to add, following the line drawn by the conciliar teachings ([+[0]+]), some guidelines "of a biblical, liturgical, ecumenical, anthropological nature" to be taken into account when reviewing or creating exercises and practices of piety, with the in order to make more alive and more meaningful the bond that unites us to the Mother of Christ and our Mother in the Communion of Saints.

30
30. The need for a biblical imprint in all forms of worship is felt today as a general postulate of Christian piety. The progress of biblical studies, the growing dissemination of Holy Scripture and, above all, the example of tradition and the intimate movement of the Spirit guide Christians of our time to increasingly use the Bible as the fundamental book of prayer and to seek in it genuine inspiration and unsurpassed models. The cult of the Blessed Virgin cannot be left outside this direction taken by Christian piety ( [+[0]+] ); On the contrary, he must draw particular inspiration from it to achieve new vigor and sure help. The Bible, in admirably proposing God's plan for the salvation of men, is all impregnated with the mystery of the Savior, and also contains, from Genesis to the Apocalypse, undoubted references to She who was the Mother and Associate of the Savior. But we would not want the biblical imprint to be limited to a diligent use of texts and symbols wisely taken from the Holy Scriptures; it carries much more; In fact, it requires that prayer formulas and compositions intended for singing take their terms and inspiration from the Bible; and it demands, above all, that the cult of the Virgin be impregnated with the great themes of the Christian message, so that, at the same time that the faithful venerate the Seat of Wisdom, they are also illuminated by the light of the divine word and induced to act according to the dictates of incarnate Wisdom.

31
31. We have already spoken of the veneration that the Church feels for the Mother of God in the celebration of the sacred Liturgy. Now, dealing with the other forms of worship and the criteria on which they should be inspired, we cannot help but remember the norm of the Constitution Sacrosanctum Concilium, which, when strongly recommending the pious exercises of the Christian people, adds: "...it is necessary that such exercises, taking into account the liturgical times, be ordered in such a way that they are in harmony with the sacred Liturgy; they are inspired in some way by it, and, given their superior nature, lead to it to the Christian people" ( [+[0]+] ). Wise rule, clear rule, whose application, however, is not easy, especially in the field of worship of the Virgin, so varied in its formal expressions: it requires, indeed, on the part of those responsible for local communities, effort, pastoral tact, perseverance; and on the part of the faithful, readiness to accept guidelines and proposals that, emanating from the genuine nature of Christian worship, sometimes involve the change of inveterate uses, in which that nature had somehow been obscured.

32
32. Due to its ecclesial nature, the cult of the Virgin reflects the concerns of the Church itself, among which the desire for the reestablishment of Christian unity stands out in our days. Piety towards the Mother of the Lord thus becomes sensitive to the concerns and purposes of the ecumenical movement, that is, it itself acquires an ecumenical imprint. And this for several reasons.

33
33. We are aware that there are not slight disagreements between the thoughts of many brothers from other Churches and ecclesial communities and Catholic doctrine "around the function of Mary in the work of salvation" ( [+[0]+] ) and, therefore, about the worship that is due to her. However, as the same power of the Almighty that overshadowed the Virgin of Nazareth ( [+[1]+] ) acts in the current ecumenical movement and fertilizes it, we wish to express our confidence that the veneration of the humble Handmaid of the Lord, in whom the Omnipotent worked wonders ( [+[2]+] ), will be, although slowly, not an obstacle but a means and meeting point for the union of all believers in Christ. We are happy, in fact, to see that a better understanding of Mary's place in the mystery of Christ and the Church, also on the part of separated brothers, makes the path to reunion easier. Just as in Cana the Virgin, with her intervention, obtained Jesus to perform the first of his miracles ([+[3]+]), so in our time She will be able to propitiate, with her intercession, the advent of the hour in which the disciples of Christ will once again find full communion in faith. And this new hope finds solace in the observation of our predecessor Leo ( [+[6]+] ).

34
34. In the cult of the Virgin, the safe and proven acquisitions of the human sciences also deserve careful consideration; This will effectively help to eliminate one of the causes of the concern that is seen in the field of the cult of the Mother of the Lord: that is, the diversity between some things in its content and the current anthropological conceptions and the profoundly changed psycho-sociological reality in which the men of our time live and act. It is observed, in fact, that it is difficult to frame the image of the Virgin, as it is presented by certain devotional literature, in the living conditions of contemporary society and in particular the conditions of women, whether in the domestic environment, where the laws and the evolution of customs tend precisely to recognize her equality and co-responsibility with men in the direction of family life; whether in the political field, where in many countries she has conquered a power of intervention in society equal to that of man; either in the social field, where it develops its activity in the most different operational sectors, leaving more and more the narrow environment of the home; the same as in the cultural field, where new possibilities of scientific research and intellectual success are offered.

35
35. First of all, the Virgin Mary has always been proposed by the Church for the imitation of the faithful not precisely because of the type of life she led and, even less so, because of the socio-cultural environment in which she developed, today surpassed almost everywhere, but because in her concrete conditions of life She adhered totally and responsibly to the will of God ([+[0]+]); because he accepted the word and put it into practice; because her action was animated by charity and the spirit of service: because, that is, she was the first and most perfect disciple of Christ: which has universal and permanent value.

36
36. Secondly, we would like to note that the difficulties to which we have alluded are in close connection with some connotations of the popular and literary image of Mary, not with her evangelical image nor with the doctrinal data determined in the slow and serious work of making the revealed word explicit; On the contrary, it should be considered normal that the Christian generations that have occurred in diverse socio-cultural frameworks, when contemplating the figure and mission of Mary - as a new and perfect Christian Woman who summarizes in herself the most characteristic situations of female life because she is Virgin, Wife, Mother - have considered the Mother of Jesus as an "exceptional model" of the feminine condition and a "clean" exemplary of evangelical life, and have expressed these feelings according to the categories and expressive modes typical of the time. The Church, when considering the long history of Marian piety, rejoices in verifying the continuity of the cultic fact, but is not linked to the representative schemes of the various cultural epochs or to the particular underlying anthropological conceptions, and understands how some expressions of worship, perfectly valid in themselves, are less suitable for men belonging to different epochs and civilizations.

37
37. Finally, we wish to emphasize that our era, like previous ones, is called to verify its own knowledge of reality with the word of God and, to limit ourselves to the case at hand, to confront its anthropological conceptions and the problems that derive from them with the figure of the Virgin as she is presented to us by the Gospel. The reading of the Holy Scriptures, done under the influence of the Holy Spirit and keeping in mind the acquisitions of human sciences and the varied situations of the contemporary world, will lead to discovering how Mary can be taken as a mirror of the hopes of the men of our time. In this way, to give an example, the contemporary woman, eager to participate with decision-making power in the community's elections, will contemplate with intimate joy Mary who, placed in dialogue with God, gives her active and responsible consent ([+[0]+]) not to the solution of a contingent problem but to the "work of the ages" as the Incarnation of the Word has been precisely called ([+[1]+]); will realize that Mary's choice of the virginal state, which in God's design disposed her to the mystery of the Incarnation, was not an act of closing herself to some of the values ​​of the married state, but rather constituted a courageous option, carried out to consecrate herself totally to the love of God; will discover with joyful surprise that Mary of Nazareth, even having abandoned herself to the will of the Lord, was something entirely different from a passively remissive woman or one of alienating religiosity, rather she was a woman who did not hesitate to proclaim that God is the vindicator of the humble and the oppressed and brings down the thrones of the powerful of the world ([+[2]+]); will recognize in Mary, who "stands out among the humble and poor of the Lord ([+[3]+]), a strong woman who knew poverty and suffering, flight and exile ([+[4]+]): all these situations that cannot escape the attention of those who want to support with an evangelical spirit the liberating energies of man and society; and Mary will not be presented to him as a mother jealously withdrawn into her own divine Son, but as a woman who with Her action favored the faith of the apostolic community in Christ ( [+[5]+] ) and whose maternal function was expanded, assuming universal dimensions on Calvary ( [+[6]+] ). promoter of justice that liberates the oppressed and of charity that helps the needy, but above all active witness of the love that builds Christ in hearts.

38
38. After having offered these guidelines, ordered to favor the harmonious development of the cult of the Mother of the Lord, we believe it is appropriate to draw attention to some erroneous cult attitudes. The Second Vatican Council has already authoritatively denounced either the exaggeration of content or forms that go so far as to falsify the doctrine, or the narrow-mindedness that obscures the figure and mission of Mary; He has also denounced some cultic devotions: the vain credulity that replaces serious effort with easy application to external practices only; the sterile and fleeting movement of feeling, so foreign to the style of the Gospel that it demands persevering and active works ([+[0]+]). We renew this deploration: they are not in harmony with the Catholic faith and therefore should not subsist in the Catholic cult. The vigilant defense against these errors and deviations will make the cult of the Virgin more vigorous and genuine: solid in its foundation, by which the study of revealed sources and attention to the documents of the Magisterium will prevail over the excessive search for novelties or extraordinary facts; objective in the historical framework, for which everything that is manifestly legendary or false must be eliminated; adapted to the doctrinal content, hence the need to avoid unilateral presentations of the figure of Mary that by insisting excessively on one element compromise the whole of the evangelical image, limpid in its motivations, for which reason any petty interest will be carefully kept away from the sanctuary.

39
39. Finally, if necessary, we would like to emphasize that the ultimate purpose of the cult of the blessed Virgin Mary is to glorify God and commit Christians to a life absolutely in accordance with his will. The children of the Church, in fact, when uniting their voices with the voice of the anonymous woman of the Gospel, glorify the Mother of Jesus, exclaiming, turned towards Him: "Blessed is the womb that carried you and the breasts that created you" ( [+[0]+] ), they will be induced to consider the grave response of the divine Master: "Blessed rather are those who hear the word of God and do it" ( [+[1]+] ). This same response, if it is a lively praise for the Virgin, as interpreted by some Holy Fathers ( [+[2]+] ) and as confirmed by the Second Vatican Council ( [+[3]+] ), also sounds for us as an admonition to live according to the commandments of God and is like an echo of other calls of the divine Master: "Not everyone who says to me: "Lord, Lord", will enter the kingdom of Heaven; but he who does the will of my Father who is in heaven" ( [+[4]+] ) and "You are my friends, if you do whatever I command you" ( [+[5]+] ).

40
40. We have indicated some principles suitable for giving new vigor to the cult of the Mother of the Lord; Now it is the responsibility of the Episcopal Conferences, of those responsible for local communities, of the different religious families to wisely restore practices and exercises of veneration of the Blessed Virgin and to support the creative impulse of those with genuine religious inspiration or pastoral sensitivity who wish to give life to new forms. However, it seems appropriate to us, even if for different reasons, to deal with two exercises that are very widespread in the West and which this Apostolic See has dealt with on several occasions: the "Angelus" and the Rosary.

42
42. We now wish, dear brothers, to dwell a little on the renewal of the pious exercise that has been called "compendium of the entire Gospel" ([+[0]+]): the Rosary. Our Predecessors have dedicated vigilant attention and diligent concern to it: they have often recommended its frequent prayer, favored its dissemination, illustrated its nature, recognized its ability to develop a contemplative prayer of praise and supplication at the same time, remembering its innate effectiveness in promoting Christian life and apostolic commitment. We also, since the first general audience of our pontificate, on July 13, 1963, have expressed our interest in the pious practice of the Rosary ([+[1]+]), and have subsequently stressed its value in multiple circumstances, some ordinary, others serious, such as when in a moment of anguish and insecurity we published the Encyclical Letter Christi Matri ([+[2]+]), so that prayers could be offered to the blessed Virgin of the Rosary to implore from God the highest good of peace ( [+[3]+] ); call that we have renewed in our Apostolic Exhortation Recurrens mensis october ([+[4]+]), in which we also commemorated the fourth centenary of the Apostolic Letter Consueverunt Romani Pontifices of our Predecessor Saint Pius V, who illustrated in it and in a certain way defined the traditional form of the Rosary ([+[5]+]).

43
43. Our assiduous interest in the Rosary has moved us to closely follow the numerous congresses dedicated in recent years to the pastoral care of the Rosary in the contemporary world: congresses promoted by associations and by men who deeply feel such devotion and in which bishops, priests, religious and lay people of proven experience and accredited ecclesial sense have taken part. Among them it is fair to remember the Children of Saint Dominic, by tradition custodians and propagators of such healthy devotion. The work of the congresses has been joined by the research of historians, carried out not to define with almost archaeological intentions the primitive form of the Rosary, but to capture its original intuition, its primary energy, its essential structure. From such congresses and investigations, the primary characteristics of the Rosary, its essential elements and their mutual relationship have appeared more clearly.

44
44. Thus, for example, the evangelical nature of the Rosary has been brought into clearer light, insofar as it draws from the Gospel the statement of the mysteries and the main formulas; It is inspired by the Gospel to suggest, starting from the joyful greeting of the Angel and the religious consent of the Virgin, the attitude with which the faithful should recite it; and continues to propose, in the harmonious succession of the Hail Marys, a fundamental mystery of the Gospel âthe Incarnation of the Wordâ in the decisive moment of the Annunciation made to Mary. Evangelical prayer therefore the Rosary, as today, perhaps more than in the past, pastors and scholars like to define it.

45
45. It has also been more easily perceived how the orderly and gradual development of the Rosary reflects the very way in which the Word of God, inserting himself with merciful determination into human vicissitudes, has carried out redemption: in it, in effect, the Rosary considers in harmonious succession the main salvific events that have been fulfilled in Christ: from the virginal conception and the mysteries of childhood to the culminating moments of Easter - the passion and the glorious resurrectionâ€”and the effects of it on the nascent Church on the day of Pentecost and on the Virgin on the day when, ending the earthly exile, she was assumed body and soul into the heavenly homeland. And it has also been observed how the triple division of the mysteries of the Rosary not only strictly adapts to the chronological order of the events, but above all reflects the scheme of the primitive announcement of the faith and proposes again the mystery of Christ in the same way that it was seen by Saint Paul in the celestial "hymn" of the Letter to the Philippians: humiliation, death, exaltation ([+[0]+]).

46
46. An evangelical prayer centered on the mystery of the redeeming Incarnation, the Rosary is, therefore, a prayer of profoundly Christological orientation. Indeed, its most characteristic element is the litany repetition in constant praise of Christ, the final term of the Angel's annunciation and the greeting of the Baptist's Mother: "Blessed is the fruit of your womb" ([+[0]+]). We will say more: the repetition of the Hail Mary constitutes the fabric on which the contemplation of the mysteries develops; The Jesus that every Hail Mary remembers is the same one that the succession of mysteries proposes to us again and again as the Son of God and the Virgin, born in a cave in Bethlehem; presented by the Mother in the Temple; young man full of zeal for his Father's things; Redeemer dying in the garden; flagellated and crowned with thorns; carrying the cross and dying on Calvary; resurrected from death and ascended to the glory of the Father to pour out the gift of the Holy Spirit. It is known that, precisely to encourage contemplation and "that the mind corresponds to the voice", it was customary in other times - and the custom has been preserved in several regions - to add to the name of Jesus, in each Hail Mary, a clause that recalls the mystery announced.

47
47. The need to emphasize, at the same time as the value of the laudatory and deprecatory element, the importance of another essential element of the Rosary has also been felt with greater urgency: contemplation. Without this, the Rosary is a body without a soul and its prayer runs the risk of becoming a mechanical repetition of formulas and of contradicting Jesus' warning: "when you pray, do not be charlatans like the pagans who believe they are heard by virtue of their loquacity" ([+[0]+]). By its nature, the recitation of the Rosary requires a calm rhythm and a reflective calm that encourages those who pray to meditate on the mysteries of the life of the Lord, seen through the Heart of the One who was closest to the Lord, and that reveal its unfathomable richness.

48
48. From contemporary reflection, the existing relationships between the Liturgy and the Rosary have finally been understood with greater precision. On the one hand, it has been highlighted how the Rosary is almost a stem germinated on the secular trunk of the Christian Liturgy, "The Psalter of the Virgin", through which the humble are associated with the "song of praise" and the universal intercession of the Church; On the other hand, it has been observed that this has occurred in a time – at the decline of the Middle Ages – in which the liturgical spirit is in decline and there is a certain distancing of the faithful from the Liturgy, in favor of a sensitive devotion to the humanity of Christ and the blessed Virgin Mary. If in recent times the desire to see the Rosary included among the liturgical expressions could arise in the minds of some, and in others, due to the concern to avoid pastoral errors of the past, an unjustified inattention towards it, today the problem has an easy solution in light of the principles of the Constitution Sacrosanctum Concilium; Liturgical celebrations and pious exercise of the Rosary should neither be contrasted nor equated ([+[0]+]). Every expression of prayer is all the more fruitful the more it preserves its true nature and the physiognomy that is its own. Confirmed, then, the preeminent value of liturgical actions, it will not be difficult to recognize that the Rosary is a pious exercise that is easily harmonized with the Sacred Liturgy. In fact, as the Liturgy has a communal nature, it is nourished by Sacred Scripture and gravitates around the mystery of Christ. Although on essentially different levels of reality, anamnesis in the Liturgy and contemplative memory in the Rosary, have as their object the same salvific events carried out by Christ. The first makes present under the veil of signs and operating in a mysterious way the "greatest mysteries of our redemption"; The second, with the pious affection of contemplation, once again evokes the same mysteries in the mind of those who pray and stimulates their will to draw from them norms of life.

Each of them has its own nature that, well understood and valued, must be reflected in the prayer, so that the Rosary expresses all its richness and variety. 51
51. In recent times, some pious exercises have been created, inspired by the Holy Rosary. We want to indicate and recommend among them those that include in the traditional scheme of the celebrations of the Word of God some elements of the Rosary to the blessed Virgin Mary, such as, for example, the meditation of the mysteries and the litany repetition of the Angel's greeting. Such elements thus acquire greater relevance when framed in the reading of biblical texts, illustrated through the homily, accompanied by pauses of silence and underlined with song. We are happy to know that such exercises have contributed to a better understanding of the spiritual riches of the Rosary itself and to revalue its practice on certain occasions and youth movements.

52
52. And now, in continuity of intention with our Predecessors, we want to strongly recommend praying the Holy Rosary as a family. The Second Vatican Council has made clear how the family, the first and vital cell of society "through the mutual piety of its members and the common prayer directed to God, offers itself as the domestic sanctuary of the Church" ([+[0]+]). The Christian family, therefore, is presented as a domestic Church ( [+[1]+] ) when its members, each within their own sphere and responsibility, together promote justice, practice works of mercy, dedicate themselves to the service of their brothers, take part in the apostolate of the local community and unite in their liturgical worship ( [+[2]+] ); and even more, supplicating prayers are raised in common to God; because if this element were to fail, the very character of the family as a domestic Church would be lacking. That is why you should make an effort to establish common prayer in family life.

53
53. In accordance with the conciliar guidelines, the Liturgy of the Hours includes precisely the family nucleus among the groups to which the common celebration of the Divine Office is best adapted: "it is finally appropriate that the family, as the domestic tabernacle of the Church, not only offer common prayers to God, but also recite appropriately some parts of the Liturgy of the Hours, in order to unite more closely with the Church" ([+[0]+]). Nothing should be left untried so that this clear indication finds growing and joyful application in Christian families.

54
54. After the celebration of the Liturgy of the Hours – the summit that domestic prayer can reach – there is no doubt that the Rosary to the Blessed Virgin must be considered one of the most excellent and effective common prayers that the Christian family is invited to pray. We want to think and we strongly hope that when a family gathering becomes a time of prayer, the Rosary will be its frequent and preferred expression. We know very well that the new living conditions of men today do not favor moments of family gathering and that, even when that takes place, many circumstances make it difficult to turn the family gathering into an occasion for prayer. Difficult, without a doubt. But it is also a characteristic of Christian action not to surrender to environmental conditions, but to overcome them; not succumb to them, but confront them. That is why families who want to fully live the vocation and spirituality of the Christian family must deploy all kinds of energy to marginalize the forces that hinder family gathering and common prayer.

55
55. Concluding these observations, testimony of the concern and esteem of this Apostolic See for the Rosary of the Blessed Virgin Mary, we nevertheless want to recommend that, when spreading this very healthy devotion, its proportions should not be altered nor should it be presented with inappropriate exclusivity: the Rosary is an excellent prayer, but the faithful should feel free, attracted to pray it, in serene tranquility, by its intrinsic beauty. .

56. Venerable Brothers: at the end of our Apostolic Exhortation we wish to summarize in summary the theological value of the cult of the Virgin and remember its pastoral effectiveness for the renewal of Christian customs.

57
57. Christ is the only way to the Father ( [+[0]+] ). Christ is the supreme model to which the disciple must conform his own conduct ([+[1]+]), until he manages to have the same feelings ([+[2]+]), live his life and possess his Spirit ([+[3]+]); This is what the Church has taught at all times and nothing in pastoral action should obscure this doctrine. But the Church, guided by the Holy Spirit and trained by secular experience, also recognizes that piety to the Blessed Virgin, subordinate to piety toward the Savior and in connection with it, has great pastoral effectiveness and constitutes a renewing force in Christian life. The reason for this effectiveness is easily understood. In fact, the multiple mission of Mary towards the People of God is a supernatural reality operating and fruitful in the ecclesial organism. And it is joyful to consider the unique aspects of said mission and see how they are oriented, each with its own effectiveness, towards the same end: to reproduce in the children the spiritual traits of the firstborn Son. We want to say that the maternal intercession of the Virgin, her exemplary holiness and the divine grace that is in Her, become a reason for hope for the human race.

Consolation, trust, hope, joy that, uniting our voice with that of the Virgin – as the Roman Liturgy pleads –, ([+[0]+]) we wish to translate into fervent praise and recognition of the Lord.
