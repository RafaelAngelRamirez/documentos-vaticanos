Canon 1 If anyone does not confess a single nature or substance of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, a single virtue and power, the consubstantial Trinity, a single deity worshiped in three subsistences or persons: let him be anathema. For one is God and Father, of whom are all things; and one is the Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things; and one is the Holy Spirit, in whom are all things.

2
Canon 2 If anyone does not confess the two nativities of the Word of God, one, in truth, before the centuries of the Father, timeless and incorporeal; and the other in the last days of the same, that he descended from heaven and was incarnated from the holy and glorious Mother of God and ever virgin Mary, and was born of her: let him be anathema.

1
Canon 1 If anyone does not confess a single nature or substance of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, a single virtue and power, the consubstantial Trinity, a single deity worshiped in three subsistences or persons: let him be anathema. For one is God and Father, of whom are all things; and one is the Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things; and one is the Holy Spirit, in whom are all things.

2
Canon 2 If anyone does not confess the two nativities of the Word of God, one, in truth, before the centuries of the Father, timeless and incorporeal; and the other in the last days of the same, that he descended from heaven and was incarnated from the holy and glorious Mother of God and ever virgin Mary, and was born of her: let him be anathema.

3
Canon 3 If someone says that one is the Word God, who performed miracles, and another is the Christ who suffered; or he says that the Word God is with the Christ who was born of a woman, or that he is in him as one in another; and does not confess one and the same Lord our Jesus Christ, the Word of God incarnate and made man, and that his are the miracles and the passions that he voluntarily endured in the flesh: let him be anathema.

4
Canon 4 If anyone says that the union of God the Word with man was made according to grace, or according to operation, or according to equal honor, or according to authority, or according to relationship, or according to affection, or according to virtue; or according to good will, as if God the Word had been pleased with man because he felt good and pious about him, as Theodore raves; or according to homonymy, according to which the Nestorians, calling God the Word Jesus and Christ, and separately naming the man Christ and Son, and manifestly saying two persons, pretend to confess one person and one Christ according only to name and honor and dignity and veneration; but it does not confess that the union of the Word God to the flesh animated with a rational and intellectual soul has been done according to the composition, or according to the person, as the holy Fathers taught; and therefore one person of him who is our Lord Jesus Christ, one of the holy Trinity: let him be anathema.

5
Canon 5 If someone does not confess that the holy and glorious ever virgin Mary is the Mother of God according to the truth, but according to a sense, or says that a pure man was born from her in whom the Word God dwelt, as Theodore and Nestorius rave; and does not confess that she is truly the Mother of God, because the Word God, who before the ages was born of the Father, in the last days was incarnated from her, and so the holy synod of Ephesus confessed her to be the Mother of God: let her be anathema.

6
Canon 6 If anyone says that our Lord Jesus Christ is one according to homonymy, or according to relationship, or according to affection; and does not confess that he is substantially one, and thus one Christ adoring him: let him be anathema.

7
Canon 7 If someone says that Christ is worshiped in two natures, so that two adorations are introduced, one special to God the Word and another special to man; or if anyone, by the suppression of the flesh, or by the confusion of deity and humanity, feigning a single nature or substance of which they are united, thus worships one Christ, and does not worship with a single adoration the Word God incarnate with his own flesh, as has been handed down from the beginning of the Church: let him be anathema.

8
Canon 8 If anyone confesses that the union has been made of two natures, of the deity and of humanity, or says that there is only one nature of God the Word incarnate, and does not understand this as the holy Fathers taught, namely, that of the divine and human nature, having been united according to the person, there has been one Christ; but with such voices he attempts to introduce a single nature or substance of the deity and flesh of Christ: let him be anathema.

9
Canon 9 If someone says: "We worship one composite Christ," not to confess a single person, but as parts of a single person, God and man, like Theodore and Nestorius; and he does not confess that the union of the Word God to the flesh has been made according to the person, and therefore the same and only one with his flesh must be worshiped: let him be anathema.

10
Canon 10 If anyone does not confess that our Lord Jesus Christ, crucified in the flesh, is true God and Lord of glory and one of the holy Trinity: let him be anathema.

11
Canon 11 If anyone does not anathematize Arius, Eunomius, Macedonian, Apollinaris, Nestorius, Eutyches and Origen, with their impious writings, and all the other heretics who have been condemned by the holy Catholic and Apostolic Church and by the predicted four holy synods, and those who felt or feel their impiety, and until death persevered in their impiety: let him be anathema.

12
Canon 12 If anyone defends the impious Theodore of Mopsuestia, who said that one is the Word God and the other is the Christ, who suffered discomfort from the passions of the soul and the desires of the flesh, and gradually turning away from the worst, became better by the progress of works, and by his conduct became immaculate, as a pure man he was baptized in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and by baptism he received the grace of divinity, and was honored with sonship; and that he must be worshiped as an image of the Word God, and that after the resurrection he became immutable in thoughts and completely impeccable; and he also said that the union of the Word of God with Christ is the same that the Apostle says of the man and the woman: "They will be two in one flesh"; and among many other crimes of blasphemy he dared to say that after the resurrection, the Lord, when he breathed on the disciples and said: "Receive the Holy Spirit," did not give them the Holy Spirit, but only figuratively breathed; and this confession, which Thomas touched his hands and his side after the resurrection, said that Thomas had not said of Christ, "My Lord and my God," but, astonished at the miracle of the resurrection, he praised God who raised Christ from the dead; and what is worse, in the exposition of the Acts of the Apostles, Theodore himself, comparing Christ with Plato and Manes and Epicurus and Marcion, said that just as each of them, having invented his own doctrine, made disciples who were called Platonists and Manichaeans and Epicureans and Marcionites, in the same way, having invented the doctrine of Christ, they call themselves Christians: if anyone, then, defends the predicted impious Theodore and his impious books, in which he vomited the predicted and innumerable other blasphemies against the great God and our Savior Jesus Christ; and do not anathematize him and his impious books, and all those who receive him or defend him, or say that he felt rightly, and those who wrote for him and his impious books, and those who feel or felt the same way, and to the end persevered in such heresy: let him be anathema.

13
Canon 13 If anyone defends the impious writings that Theodoret impiously wrote against the right faith and against the first holy synod of Ephesus and holy Cyril and his twelve chapters; and everything he wrote in favor of Theodore and Nestorius: be anathema.

14
Canon 14 If anyone defends the letter that Ibas is said to have written to Maris the Persian, which denies that the Word God was incarnated from the holy Mother of God and ever virgin Mary, and became man; but he says that a pure man was born from her, which he calls the temple, so that one is the Word God and another is the man; and he accuses the holy Cyril, who preached the right faith of Christians, of being a heretic, and reviles him as if he had written the same thing as Apollinaris; and rebukes the first holy synod of Ephesus, as if it had deposed Nestorius without trial or examination; and calls the twelve chapters of holy Cyril impious and contrary to the right faith, and defends Theodore and Nestorius and their impious doctrines and books: if anyone, then, defends the foretold letter, and does not anathematize it and those who defend it, and those who say that it is right, or part of it, and those who wrote or write in its favor or in favor of the impieties contained in it, and dared to defend it her or the impieties that are in her under the name of the holy Fathers or of the holy synod of Chalcedon, and in this they persevered to the end: let her be anathema.
